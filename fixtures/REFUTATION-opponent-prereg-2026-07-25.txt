### VERDICT:  EXECUTE-WITH-AMENDMENTS — but not the experiment as written.
################################################################################

  The prereg has TWO independent arguments bolted together, and they have opposite
  standing. §4.3a (the deployed pitcher form was never in the bake-off; it is a greedy
  sequence of pairwise-vs-incumbent changes) is CORRECT, verified in code, and if
  anything UNDERSTATED — an independent audit of the five post-bake-off changes found
  exactly one of them ran bidirectional out-of-time, and that one tested a form that was
  not shipped. A form re-selection is overdue on that ground alone. §2 (the form ranking
  is suspect because opponent-blind candidates competed on ABSORBING the missing
  opponent) is NOT SUPPORTED. Its second named instance contradicts its own cited
  evidence, and the absorption prize it postulates is, measured on the bake-off's own
  metrics at the bake-off's own grain, +0.0245 Pearson for pitchers and +0.0007 for
  hitters as a CEILING at perfect absorption, of which ≤6% / ≤18% is reachable by any
  own-ratings form — and exactly 0.000 on top-N overlap and value-regret, the two
  decision metrics. Worse, the ESTIMAND itself does not survive identification: the
  matchup cell is a deterministic function of (own hand, side), and once the cell×season
  level is held at the league grain, the response of PITCHER BB — the largest channel in
  the survey, 62.6% — falls from −0.252 to −0.010 with a CI covering zero. The scales
  the prereg proposes to fit are, for the biggest channel, a platoon dummy wearing an
  opponent-rating label, and G1 cannot tell the difference because both mechanisms
  produce the same cell levels. EXECUTE the §4.3a form-selection debt as its own,
  separate, non-atomic project. Do NOT execute P1→P4 as specified: replace P1's gates,
  fix the §1 default, and re-scope P4.

### THE SINGLE STRONGEST ARGUMENT AGAINST EXECUTING IT
################################################################################

  THE OPPONENT RESPONSE IS NOT IDENTIFIED AS A RESPONSE TO OPPONENT QUALITY. IT IS
  IDENTIFIED AS A 4-LEVEL PLATOON CONTRAST, AND THE PREREG PROPOSES TO FIT IT AS THE
  FORMER AND EXTRAPOLATE IT TO TOURNAMENT POOLS.

  In tools/opponent-term-channel-survey.ts the regressor is `oppVal(d, r)` (line 233):
  the (season × cell) mean opposing rating. The cell is `${r.T}|${r.B}` (line 201) —
  pitcher hand × batter hand. For a pitcher, `T` is his `throws` and `B` is `o.side`;
  for a hitter, `T` is `o.side` and `B` is his resolved bat-hand. So THE CELL IS A
  DETERMINISTIC FUNCTION OF (own hand, side). The regressor therefore takes exactly 28
  distinct values over 719/789 rows (M1 §0, measured), is perfectly collinear with a
  4-level platoon dummy, and the "fitted response … CI [−0.322, −0.263]" is a slope
  through 28 points, not 719.

  The discriminating test: rebuild at the (LEAGUE × season) grain, where opposing quality
  genuinely varies ACROSS the five leagues inside the same (season, cell) — 132 design
  points instead of 28 — and ask whether the response survives a (cell × season) fixed
  effect. If the mechanism is opponent quality, it must. Measured (M1 §1, M2 §B):

     channel        raw slope   +cell×season FE      95% CI (card cluster)   surviving
     PITCHER BB        -0.252            -0.010          [-0.066, +0.035]          4%   COVERS 0
     PITCHER K         +0.676            +0.170          [+0.067, +0.254]         25%   CI-clear
     HITTER BB         +0.233            +0.174          [+0.071, +0.278]         75%   CI-clear
     HITTER K          -0.406            -0.172          [-0.293, -0.069]         42%   CI-clear
     HITTER HR         +0.084            +0.085          [+0.037, +0.131]        101%   CI-clear
     PITCHER HR        -0.082            -0.111          [-0.136, -0.088]        136%   CI-clear

  PITCHER BB — the survey's #1 channel (62.6% of residual mean-square, the largest
  composition gap in the whole survey at 23 EYE points, the channel that carries the
  stuffAug verdict) — HAS NO MEASURABLE RESPONSE TO OPPOSING EYE once the platoon cell is
  held. This is not low power: the within-cell sd of opposing EYE is 3.21 rating points
  (24% of total, M2 §A) across 132 cells, and the CI [−0.066, +0.035] EXCLUDES the raw
  −0.252 and the model's own pricing −0.380 by a wide margin. Nor is it generic
  attenuation: PITCHER HR resolves a CI-clear slope on a SMALLER within-cell signal, and
  both HR channels survive at ≥100%. The instrument works. Pitcher BB specifically fails.

  WHAT THAT MEANS. Whatever produces the pitcher-BB same/opposite level gap is a property
  of the MATCHUP CELL, not a dose-response to the opposition's walk-drawing ability. The
  survey's own text names the two live alternatives and then does not test either:
  manager-selected exposure (§"A CAVEAT THAT APPLIES TO EVERY NUMBER ABOVE"), and — not
  named at all — the possibility that the engine applies a platoon adjustment ON TOP of
  the hand-split ratings. The plan's mechanism claim that "no mechanism makes the
  rating→outcome CONVERSION hand-dependent" (REBUILD_MATCHUP_CHANNEL_PLAN.md:342-344) is
  ASSERTED, never tested. M1 is the first test of it, and for pitcher BB it fails.

  A term fitted on the raw contrast and shipped will price opponents at ~0.76× the
  model's own curve on pitcher BB. In a tournament pool whose opposing EYE sits 20 points
  off the league frame that is 20 × 0.380 × 0.76 ≈ 5.8 uBB/600 ≈ 0.007 wOBA of invented
  signal, on a channel where the honest estimate is 0.03× [−0.09, +0.17]. That is larger
  than any correction the program currently ships, and G6 (extrapolation safety) checks
  boundedness and monotonicity — not whether the slope is real.

  AMENDMENT THAT FIXES IT (mandatory before any fit):
    A-1. REPLACE G3's identification test. A channel's scale may move off its default ONLY
         if it is CI-clear on the WITHIN-(season × cell) variation at the LEAGUE grain —
         i.e. the M1/M2 estimator — not on the raw contrast. Report both numbers for every
         channel. On today's evidence that admits at most PITCHER K, HITTER BB, HITTER K
         and the two HR channels, and EXCLUDES PITCHER BB.
    A-2. Add a pre-registered PLACEBO: fit the same S1 scale with the opposing rating
         REPLACED by a pure same/opposite indicator scaled to the same cell means. If the
         indicator fits as well as the rating-based term (it will, on 28 points), the
         rating parameterisation is not doing the work and the term must not be
         extrapolated on the rating axis.

  ANSWER: the MECHANISM is real, the MAGNITUDE is off by roughly two orders of

  ── 1a. WOULD THE BAKE-OFF'S METRICS REWARD ABSORPTION? Partly, and far less than §2 needs.

  half right. The absorption is a level effect PER CELL, not a global level, so Pearson
  is NOT blind to it by affine-invariance. But the size is the question, and it is
  measurable. M3 does it at the harness's exact grain (loadWindow SUMS seasons into one
  row per card×side — evaluate.ts:34/54/62 all work on that object) with the harness's
  own metric code (src/training/metrics.ts), on the DEPLOYED forms:

  Those Δ are the CEILING — what a form would gain if it absorbed the cell structure
  PERFECTLY. The reachable fraction is bounded by how well own ratings can express the
  cell level at that grain. Weighted R² of the cell level on [1, five/four own ratings,
  their squares] — a design MORE flexible than any deployed channel curve:
      HITTER 0.179     PITCHER 0.060
  So the realistically reachable Pearson prize is ≈ 0.0245 × 0.060 ≈ +0.0015 (pitchers)
  and ≈ 0.0007 × 0.179 ≈ +0.0001 (hitters).

  READ. §2's claim — "a form search will select whichever candidate BENDS ENOUGH TO
  ABSORB IT. Flexibility then scores well for a reason unrelated to correctness" — is
  quantitatively FALSE on the hitter side (prize ≈ 1e-4 Pearson, and NEGATIVE on top-N
  overlap) and marginal on the pitcher side (≈1.5e-3, versus decision margins of ~1e-3
  to 7e-3 in the actual comparisons that were run). It is EXACTLY ZERO on top-N overlap
  and value-regret in both roles. Every conclusion of the form "closing the omission can
  change the ranking" rests on a prize that the harness's decision metrics cannot see.

### LINE OF ATTACK 3 — THE DEFAULT-SCALE-1.00 RULE (§1)
################################################################################

  IT IS NOT CONSERVATIVE. It is the least conservative of the three options (0, 1.00, or
  "do not ship this channel"), and there is now a concrete case.

  §1: "A channel whose scale the data cannot pin STAYS at 1.00. It is not dropped, not
  special-cased, and not set to zero."

  Note first what the prereg itself tabulates: three of four measured ratios EXCLUDE 1.00
  (0.76 [0.69,0.85], 0.60 [0.51,0.68], 1.30 [1.10,1.52]). A default is supposed to be the
  value the data is silent about; here the data is loudly not-1.00 wherever it speaks.

  THE CONSTRUCTED CASE — PITCHER BB, and it is not hypothetical:
    · the model's own hitter-BB curve prices an opposing EYE point at 0.380 uBB/600;
    · scale 1.00 ships exactly that;
    · the identification-honest estimate is 0.03× with CI [−0.09, +0.17] (M2 §B), which
      EXCLUDES 1.00 by a factor of ~6 on the CI's own upper edge;
    · G3 as written would leave it at 1.00, because the FE'd estimate is "not CI-clear".
  So the rule takes the ONE channel where the honest evidence says the opponent response
  is near zero and ships the largest possible response for it. Today that channel is
  opponent-blind and costs nothing in-frame; after P4 it would inject ~0.38 uBB/600 per
  opposing-EYE point into every out-of-frame pool. At a 20-point pool gap that is ~7.6
  uBB/600 ≈ 0.009 wOBA of pure fabrication, on the channel with the LARGEST measured
  composition gap in the corpus and therefore the largest lever.

  The asymmetry the rule ignores: the status quo (opponent-blind) is a KNOWN, MEASURED,
  in-frame-calibrated state whose out-of-frame error the shipped correction layer was
  fitted against. Scale 1.00 is an UNMEASURED state. "Not dropped, not special-cased" is
  a governance virtue, not a statistical one — declining to estimate a parameter is not
  the same as asserting it equals the largest value on the table.

  AMENDMENT:
    A-6. The default for an unpinned channel is 0.00 (= the status quo, opponent-blind),
         NOT 1.00. A scale moves off 0.00 only under A-1. This is the only default that
         preserves the property the program actually needs: that an unresolved question
         leaves behaviour unchanged, so the shipped correction layer stays valid for it.
         If 1.00 is retained for governance reasons, then §1 must additionally require
         that any channel left at default be reported as a NEW published residual under
         G8 — which, per G8's own rule, would need an explicit STOP.

### LINE OF ATTACK 4 — DOUBLE-COUNTING WITH THE EXISTING CORRECTION LAYER
################################################################################

  4a. THE PREREG'S DISTINCTNESS CLAIM IS TRUE IN CODE. Verified:
    · src/scoring-core/hit-tail.ts:13-17 — "the correction must be exactly the identity at
      zero pool gap — which it is BY CONSTRUCTION: every strength is λ·w(g) with g = k−1
      … league/unrestricted pool ⇒ k ≤ 1 ⇒ g = 0 ⇒ no-op."
    · src/model/matchup.ts:38 — `matchupShift = buildFrameShift(opp.train, opp.pool)`, and
      the matched-legs construction (server.ts trainingMeans comment) collapses the
      in-frame gap to ~0.
    · The independent audit in fixtures/hitter-residual-channels-2026-07-25.txt Part 7
      confirms it empirically ("applyHitTail is the EXACT IDENTITY" in frame).
  §7's assertion stands. This part of the prereg is correct and well-founded.

  4b. BUT G7 IS NOT A GATE — IT IS A RIDGE. This is the harder question and the prereg
  does not address it. The shipped ramps' coordinate is g = μ_train/μ_pool − 1, a function
  of the OPPOSING FIELD's mean. An S1 opponent term's input is the opposing field's mean
  predicted rate. OUT OF FRAME these are the same stimulus, up to a monotone map. So after
  P4, tournament behaviour is governed by (opponent scale) × (ramp λ) along a ridge, and
  the only data that can separate them is the tournament corpus — which the program's own
  record says is 3-runnings-thin on Bronze-t, cannot resolve elite spread (τ_elite → 1e-9,
  "requires 15–20 runnings/tier"), and whose deconvolved-spread instrument already FAILED
  once. G7 says "refit … then the full C6 gates + Scorecard v2, no worse than the shipped
  baseline." A ridge refit will meet "no worse than" by construction — it has a free
  parameter to spend — while the SPLIT between the model term and the ramp is arbitrary.
  The consequence is not a bad scorecard; it is that after P4 nobody can say which part of
  the out-of-frame behaviour is the model and which is the patch, and the next retrain
  will land somewhere else on the ridge.

  AMENDMENT:
    A-7. G7 must add an IDENTIFIABILITY condition, not just a non-regression one: the
         opponent scale must be FROZEN at its in-frame (league) estimate and NOT refit on
         tournament data; only the ramp λ may move. And G7 must report the ramp's
         sensitivity to the opponent scale — dλ/d(scale) over the scale's CI. If a ±1 CI
         of the scale moves λ by more than its own need-SE, the two are not separable and
         the event must not ship both.
    A-8. Pre-register, before the refit, what a "no worse than the shipped baseline"
         scorecard would look like under the NULL that the opponent term does nothing but
         re-parameterise the ramps. If the predicted null scorecard is indistinguishable
         from the predicted success scorecard, G7 is not a test.

### LINE OF ATTACK 5 — G1's 15% BAR
################################################################################

  G1 IS AN IN-SAMPLE GOODNESS-OF-FIT DIAGNOSTIC DRESSED AS A GATE. It measures the share
  of residual mean-square explained by matchup-cell FE after S1. But the S1 scale is a
  single free parameter per channel fitted, by least squares, on a design whose dominant
  structure IS the cell level. Asking whether the fit removed the thing it was fitted to
  remove is not a test of mechanism.

  · CAN A TERM PASS G1 AND STILL MAKE THE MODEL WORSE? Yes, and this is the live case. A
    pure platoon dummy — scale × (a same/opposite indicator) — passes G1 outright, because
    it IS the cell level. The rating-parameterised term is, on 28 collinear design points,
    numerically indistinguishable from it (M1). It then extrapolates on the RATING axis to
    tournament pools where the platoon mixture is different and the rating levels are far
    outside the fitted domain. G1 passes; G6 checks boundedness and monotonicity, not
    whether the axis is real; and the model ships a slope that M2 says is 0.03× on the
    largest channel.
  · CAN A CORRECT TERM FAIL G1? Yes. If part of the cell level is NOT opponent quality —
    manager selection, or an engine-side platoon adjustment — a CORRECT opponent term will
    leave that part behind and can miss 15%. G1 then kills a correct term for being honest.
    G1 is wrong in both directions.
  · IS THE 15% BAR EVEN THE BINDING CONSTRAINT? No: G2 is a NON-REGRESSION gate ("no worse
    than the current model"), so P1 as specified CANNOT KILL THE PROJECT — a term that does
    nothing passes G2 trivially and, per the argument above, passes G1 too. This directly
    contradicts §5 ("This phase alone can end the project") and §10 (whose stated null —
    "S1 absorbs little AND out-of-time metrics do not move" — is not what G1/G2 test). The
    prereg's stopping rule and its gates disagree with each other.

  A BETTER GATE — proposed, and cheap:
    G1′ (LEAVE-ONE-CELL-OUT + WITHIN-CELL). Two conditions, both required:
      (i) OUT-OF-CELL: fit the eight scales with one matchup cell HELD OUT; predict the
          held-out cell's level; repeat for all four cells. A term that has learned an
          opponent response predicts an unseen cell's level from that cell's opposing
          profile. A term that has learned a platoon dummy cannot — it has no value for
          the held-out level. Pre-commit the bar in the deliverable's own units: the
          out-of-cell prediction error must be below need-SE.
      (ii) WITHIN-CELL: the fitted scale must be reproduced, within its CI, by the
          M1/M2 within-(season × cell) estimator at the league grain (A-1).
    Failure of (i) in PITCHER BB and PITCHER K — the two the prereg nominates as its
    stop condition — is then a real stop, on a real quantity.

  AMENDMENT:
    A-9. Replace G1 with G1′. Replace G2's "no worse than" with a two-sided condition:
         the term must be no worse on the decision metrics AND must show a pre-committed
         positive movement somewhere, or P1 reports the null and P2 does not run.

### LINE OF ATTACK 6 — THE JOINT-CONVERGENCE SCHEME (§4.4, G5)
################################################################################

  6a. CONVERGENCE IS NOT GUARANTEED, AND CONVERGENCE WOULD NOT BE EVIDENCE. §4.4 fits
  hitters against the current pitcher form, then pitchers against the new hitter form,
  and iterates. That is NOT block-coordinate descent: the hitter step minimises the
  HITTER SSE and the pitcher step minimises the PITCHER SSE. There is no single objective
  that both steps decrease, so the standard monotonicity argument does not apply. What is
  actually being run is a fixed-point iteration of a general map T(hit, pit). Such maps
  can cycle, diverge, or admit multiple fixed points, and nothing in the prereg bounds the
  map's contraction. Even a converged point is not a stationary point of any joint
  likelihood — so "it converged" carries no statistical content whatever. G5 treats
  non-convergence as a STOP and convergence as a pass; only the first of those is
  informative.

  6b. THE 0.02 CRITERION IS TIGHTER THAN THE ESTIMATION PRECISION. The scales' CIs, on the
  prereg's own table, span 0.16–0.42 (pit BB [0.69,0.85]; pit K [0.86,1.38]; hit K
  [1.10,1.52]). A convergence criterion of "every scale moving < 0.02" is 8–20× tighter
  than the uncertainty in the scales themselves. It is a numerical-precision test, and it
  will pass for any stable arithmetic — including a fixed point that is statistically
  meaningless.

  6c. THE ENTANGLEMENT IS SMALLER THAN THE MACHINERY. In-frame, the opposing input under
  S1 reduces to the mean predicted opposing rate PER CELL — 4 numbers per role per channel
  (28 with seasons). The "joint fit" is therefore an iteration over ~28 constants, not a
  genuinely coupled functional problem. P3 is engineering built for a problem the corpus
  does not pose.

  AMENDMENT:
    A-10. Drop the fixed-point iteration. Fit both roles' curves and both roles' scales in
          ONE weighted least-squares problem over the stacked hitter+pitcher rows (the
          harness already passes `opp` to BakeoffModel.fit — bakeoff.ts:63 — and
          fitMatchupK already does exactly this stacking, forms.ts:724-733). A single
          objective gives a unique optimum, an honest df count, and makes G5 unnecessary.
          If the iteration is kept, G5's criterion must be stated in the SCALE's own SE
          units (e.g. < 0.1 × SE), and multi-start from every candidate form must be
          required with the fixed-point SET published, not just the one reached.

### NEW OBJECTIONS THE PREREG DOES NOT CONSIDER
################################################################################

  N-1. THE SURVEY DIAGNOSES WITH f(mean) AND THE PREREG PROPOSES mean-of-f. The "pricing
    ratio" compares (slope of the residual on the CELL-MEAN opposing rating) against (the
    local DERIVATIVE of the complementary curve evaluated AT that mean) —
    tools/opponent-term-channel-survey.ts:207 `dSlope(..., x0)` with `x0 = wmean(rs, oppVal)`.
    For a LOG curve (hit.bb, hit.k are both log) f′ = b/x, so f′′ ≠ 0 and by Jensen the
    average marginal over the opposing DISTRIBUTION is not f′(mean). The 0.60–0.76×
    "discount" on the log channels is therefore partly a curvature artifact of evaluating
    at the mean, not an opponent-pricing fact. More importantly for the design: S1 as
    proposed enters via "the model's own predicted rate for the OPPOSING CARD" — i.e.
    E[f(x)], not f(E[x]) — so the survey's ratios are NOT the calibration target for S1's
    scale, and §1's "default 1.00 = what the model already says that rating point is worth"
    is comparing two different quantities. AMENDMENT: recompute every ratio as the
    exposure-weighted mean of the complementary curve's LOCAL slope over the actual
    opposing distribution, not the slope at the mean, before any scale is defaulted.

  N-2. S1's OPERAND IS UNSPECIFIED, AND FOR TWO CHANNELS IT CANNOT BE A PROBABILITY.
    "Odds-ratio / log5 … in log-odds space" is defined on probabilities. `predictPitching`
    /`predictHitting` return per-600 COUNTS (K ≈ 130/600, BB ≈ 50/600), and the H channel's
    quantity is a per-BIP rate whose denominator BIP is DERIVED from BB+K+HR
    (curves.ts hRate; forms.ts:228, :276). A log5 on H therefore feeds back into the BIP
    chain that produced its own denominator. The prereg never states which quantity log5
    operates on for which channel. This is not pedantry: it determines G6's boundedness
    answer and it determines whether the eight scales are even on comparable footing.
    AMENDMENT: §4.2 must state, per channel, the exact operand (rate/600 as a probability;
    per-BIP rate; or the count) and the baseline, and G6 must be evaluated on that operand.

  N-3. §9's OWN CAVEAT INVALIDATES G3's SECOND CONDITION AND HALF OF G2. §9 states:
    "Out-of-time is not out-of-card on this corpus: the same cards recur across seasons
    with residuals correlating r = 0.94, so cross-season agreement is close to arithmetic."
    G3 then requires scales to be "sign-consistent on held-out seasons" — a condition that,
    by §9's own statement, is satisfied by construction and adds no information. The same
    caveat is what the kcurve refutation §11 used to demote a headline result ("agreeing is
    the null, not the finding"). The prereg records the caveat and then writes gates that
    depend on the thing the caveat invalidates. AMENDMENT: delete "sign-consistent on
    held-out seasons" from G3 (it is not evidence) and replace it with sign-consistency
    across LEAGUES within season (33 league-seasons, genuinely different opposition
    populations) — which is also what the within-cell test in A-1 exploits.

  N-4. THE MATCHUP CELL IS NOT ORTHOGONAL TO ROLE, AND ROLE IS NOT IN THE MODEL. The
    kcurve refutation §7(b) records that same-side exposure varies by role (starters vs
    non-starters), and §3 of the survey records same-side BF share by Stuff quintile as
    33/32/28/63/30% — a NON-MONOTONE spike at Q4. An opponent term fitted on cell means
    absorbs whatever else is cell-shaped, including role-driven usage. The prereg's §8
    "Collinearity with own ratings" risk names hand and card composition but not ROLE.
    AMENDMENT: add role (SP / RP / CL) as a pre-registered control in every identification
    regression, and report the scales with and without it.

  N-5. G8's LEDGER IS ALREADY VIOLATED BY THE PREREG'S OWN §1. G8: "Six published residuals
    stand. No seventh without an explicit STOP." §1's rule leaves any unpinned channel at
    scale 1.00 — which is a shipped, unvalidated behaviour on that channel, i.e. exactly
    the kind of thing the ledger exists to count. Under A-6's default (0.00) there is no
    new residual; under 1.00 there are up to eight. The prereg cannot hold both G8 and §1.

  N-6. NOT AN OBJECTION — A CHECK THAT PASSED. I tested §3's structural exclusion of
    HITTER XBH against the DATA rather than the code, since the code's 0.25 is a modelling
    choice the prereg is otherwise re-opening. Measured (M4, window 2042+2043, 149 rows /
    67 cards): pitcher XBH share of non-HR hits has BF-weighted mean 0.2489 (the hard-coded
    0.25 is right), sd 0.0148, range [0.156, 0.333] — it DOES vary — but its weighted
    correlation with every pitcher rating is |r| ≤ 0.064 (hrr −0.047, pbabip +0.016,
    stu +0.064, con +0.054). There is no pitcher rating that predicts it. The exclusion is
    correct on the data, not merely on the code. §3 survives.
