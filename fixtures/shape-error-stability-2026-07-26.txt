  NEGATIVE one — re-cutting the metric to 14 would sharpen what it MEANS, not what it RESOLVES.

  masquerading as a shape effect will not. No claim of replication is made beyond that.

  legitimately carry it. Measured both ways:

  §4c MECHANISM PROBE — IS THE SHAPE CONTRAST A PROPORTIONAL SHADOW OF THE LEVEL DRIFT?
  The level column above is not noise: cards improve every season, so a model fit on an EARLY
  window over-predicts a LATER block and one fit on a late window under-predicts an earlier one
  (the standing league-frame result). That level is absorbed by the anchor — but only if it is
  ADDITIVE. If the frame acts MULTIPLICATIVELY on wOBA, the same proportional miss produces a
  LARGER ABSOLUTE error for the high-wOBA cards, and hi-EYE/hi-POW cards ARE the high-wOBA cards.
  That would manufacture a shape contrast whose SIGN follows the level's sign — exactly the
  pattern §2 found — out of a pure level phenomenon with no shape content at all.
  Test: rescale the predicted line by the pool-wide multiplicative level λ = Σw·wOBApred ÷
  Σw·wOBAobs (one scalar per window, fit on the pool, NOT on the cell) and re-measure. Removing
  an ADDITIVE level cannot change a contrast at all — the difference of two group means already
  cancels it — so any movement here is attributable to the multiplicative form alone.

  READING IT. "overlap" = (K − false picks)/K, the project's standing top-N metric, put on a RATE
  so the cuts are comparable. "cost/slot" = [observed wOBA of the true top-K − observed wOBA of
  the model's top-K] ÷ K — the value each roster slot gives up, again comparable across cuts.
  "paired gap" pairs each missed card with the false pick that displaced it (both ordered by
  observed value) and averages the difference — how far the displaced cards sit from the ones
  chosen. "true rank of false" = where the model's mistaken picks actually belong; a number just
  past K means near-misses, a large number means real errors. ◀ marks the roster-relevant cut.

################################################################################
# §8 — WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
################################################################################

  · No correction is proposed, fitted, or wired. §6's coefficients are descriptive statistics of
    the residual surface, printed because the STABLE branch would need the surface characterised;
    they are not candidate terms and no adoption decision follows from them.
  · The three window estimates share most of their CARDS (§0e). Agreement across them is NOT
    independent replication; §5 is the only out-of-card read here and it halves the sample.
  · The 2x2 cell is a construction. A tercile grid or a fitted surface would name a related but
    not identical region (§6a shows the surface so the construction-dependence is visible). At
    20-40 cards per cell the boundaries cannot be sharp.
  · Every number is at the deployed variant policy and the deployed wOBA weights, held fixed.
    Per-window weight re-derivation was deliberately suppressed so it could not masquerade as
    instability; that also means the level readings in §4 are not the production levels.
  · minPA is a fit knob AND a population knob (§3 arm A vs arm B). Arm B's cells change the card
    population, so a difference between the arms is informative but neither arm alone is "the"
    threshold effect.
  · §7's cut correction is an OBSERVATION ABOUT THE METRIC, not a change to it. Nothing in
    src/training/evaluate.ts was touched and topN=26 is still the production default; the
    top-14 figures here are computed inside this tool. Whether the incumbent metric should be
    re-cut is a separate decision on separate evidence, and this artifact does not make it.
  · The roster-relevant cut is the MODAL split (14/12, 33 of 48 configs), not a universal one —
    15 configs roster a different number of hitters, and a pitcher-side cut would be 12, not 14.
    This artifact measures the HITTER side only, so no pitcher cut is claimed.
