┌─ VERDICT ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

TWO CORRECTIONS COME FIRST, BOTH STATED BEFORE ANY NUMBER.

(1) SURVIVORSHIP — handled by STRATIFICATION, not bounding. Run depth is DIRECTLY OBSERVABLE here:
    exactly one pitcher starts each team-game, so a team's games = ΣGS_1 over its rows. No proxy is
    used or needed, and the conditional distribution is exact. Bounding would have been strictly
    weaker. It matters: the deepest 10% of teams supply 26.3% of all hitter PA and the deepest
    quarter supply 50.0%. And the shape MOVES with depth — N_eff 10.68 at 1–5 games rising to 12.28
    at 15+. Crucially it moves in BOTH directions depending on the quantity: it makes the lineup-
    curve failure LARGER, and it turns an apparent rotation-curve failure into a PASS (short runs
    censor SP4/SP5 out of existence). Every verdict below is taken from the DEEP stratum.

(2) FORMAT — the five folders are NOT interchangeable, and each was resolved against
    data/tournaments/ independently (§0b). Four map to budget_mode:none, era-2010/park-1 NEUTRAL,
    DH formats with min_starters 5, and they measure ALIKE on every quantity here despite spanning
    card-value windows ≤69 / ≤89 / uncapped (§1c) — those four, and only those four, are pooled.
    The fifth, Early Gold, is env-modified (era-1920/park-169), NO-DH, min_starters 4, and is a
    different regime on the pitching side (rotation BF share 88.4% vs 66.2%); it is reported
    SEPARATELY throughout and never pooled.
    SCOPE HOLE, up front: all five are budget_mode:none with total_cap null. The corpus contains NO
    cap and NO slots format. A budget forces a few expensive cards plus cheap filler, which
    reallocates playing time across a roster — the exact estimand — so NOTHING here transfers to
    capped or slots play, and cap/slots is the main consumer of these parameters.

WHAT THE DATA SUPPORTS (3 of the model's assumptions).
  • rotationStarts — the format-derived rotation-slot curve, and the only usage component that was
    DERIVED rather than hand-set. At depth it is confirmed in both a 5-man DH format and a 4-man
    no-DH one: neutral 15+ games 0.237 0.221 0.203 0.184 0.153 vs rotationStarts(bo7,k5)
    0.224 0.216 0.204 0.191 0.164 (max per-slot deviation 0.012); Early Gold 15+ 0.267 0.256 0.247 0.193 vs
    rotationStarts(bo5,k4) 0.277 0.264 0.252 0.206 (deviation 0.013). rotationDecay = 0 is
    correspondingly vindicated: no extra manual tilt is needed on top of the format curve. CAVEAT:
    `bestOf` is NOT observable in the export, so the comparator was CHOSEN as the best-fitting
    member of {3,5,7} — the FORM is supported, the bestOf ARGUMENT is not verified (§3a).
  • THE ~9-MAN EVERYDAY CORE. 9.5 hitters clear half a full-time share (10.1 at depth) — the model's
    nine-man lineup is the right description of the CORE. What it lacks is everything below it.
  • THE BULLPEN PREMIUM'S LEVEL, under the leverage-weighted reading only: realised RP1 = 0.322
    and RP2 = 0.211 of leverage-weighted innings against the model's 0.278 / 0.167.

WHAT THE DATA REFUTES.
  • THE LINEUP CURVE'S SUPPORT — the largest and most consequential miss, and the one survivorship
    makes WORSE. The model gives PA to exactly 9 hitters and zero to the rest; the bench enters only
    through fullStrengthShare, worth 4.44% of lineup PA. Realised in the neutral pool: the top
    nine take 84.5% of hitter PA (79.4% at depth), 13.2 hitters get a PA (13.7 at depth), 11.9
    clear a quarter-share, and N_eff is 11.39 pooled / 12.28 at depth against a model value of
    8.93 — at most 9.77 even if the entire substitution allowance is spread perfectly over
    the bench. The model under-spreads by ≈2.5 effective hitters at the depth that matters.
  • rotationShare 0.62 IS TOO LOW, AND IS NOT ONE NUMBER. Neutral pool 66.2% (+4.2%); Early Gold
    88.4% (+26.4%). The swingman attribution band is under 1pp wide, so this is not an
    artefact of how BF was split. Early Gold's starters carry BF-weighted stamina
    88.4 vs 74.7 and go 34.5 BF/start vs 25.3 — the driver is which cards the format makes
    ELIGIBLE, so a single global rotationShare cannot be right across formats.
  • THE BULLPEN'S FLAT TAIL, under BOTH readings of the parameter — and the parameter turns out to be
    AMBIGUOUS, which is the real finding (§3b). `bullpenLeverage` multiplies plain `bullpenBF`, but
    the code comment calls it 'leverage-weighted'. Read as plain BF ranked by leverage ROLE,
    realised is FLAT (0.14 0.14 0.14 0.14 0.14 0.14 0.12; RP1/RP7 = 1.12) and the 2.5× premium is
    refuted. Read as leverage-weighted innings, the premium is fine but the tail keeps falling
    (0.154 0.118 0.087 0.062 0.037; RP1/RP7 = 8.7 vs 2.50) and the flat tail is refuted.
    Exactly one of the two claims survives under each reading, never the same one. Underneath, the
    leverage index itself declines smoothly 2.43 → 0.43 from closer to 7th man with no flat
    region, and a [premium, premium, flat…] shape cannot represent that at any two values.
  • fullStrengthShare 0.6's IMPLICATION — a FORM failure, not a value failure. The rule requires the
    nine to take 95.6% of lineup starts. Realised is 86.3% pooled and 80.5% at depth,
    inverting to f0 = -0.24 and -0.75 — outside [0,1], so NO value of f0 can express the
    observed churn. Real rosters rotate a 14-man batting group; 'nine starters, one occasionally
    missing' is the wrong shape. This is confounded (rest / platooning / blowouts all enter, §4b),
    so it refutes the RULE, not any particular value — and it says bench depth is used MORE than the
    model allows, not less, so it does not undercut the reason offense.ts values a bench at all.
  • THE ORDER TILT'S MAGNITUDE (minor). Realised slot-1:slot-9 PA ratio 1.61 vs the model's 1.32;
    the direction is right, the steepness is understated. Small next to the support failure.

WHAT THIS CORPUS CANNOT ANSWER.
  • platoonCapture ρ = 0.80: NOT MEASURABLE, and no proxy is offered. Every realised statistic in
    the export is an undifferentiated total; the vL/vR columns are RATINGS, not splits. Only the
    handedness SUPPLY is measurable (§4a) and supply is not capture.
  • fullStrengthShare as a VALUE — only its shape is testable, and its shape fails.
  • Anything about CAPPED or SLOTS formats — none are present.
  • Anything about what a roster COULD be — these rosters are ownership-shaped.

NOTHING WAS FITTED. No parameter is proposed, no default touched. A calibration built on this would
need its own pre-registration, and would have to be per-format at least for rotationShare.

There is NO by-opponent-handedness split of any realised statistic, and no game-by-game or
by-lineup-slot breakdown. Everything below is built from the columns above and nothing else.

IP is printed base-3 in the fraction ("13.2" = 13⅔ innings). Every innings share below is computed
from BF, a plain integer, so that trap cannot contaminate a result.

THE EXPORT LISTS ONLY CARDS THAT APPEARED. Rows with G = G_1 = G_2 = 0: 0 of 37,992. So an unused
bench card is INVISIBLE here, and every count below is an APPEARANCE count, never a roster count.
This does not touch any statistic in this file (all are conditional on PA>0 or GS>0, and a zero-PA
card contributes nothing to N_eff), but it does mean no claim here is a claim about roster
CONSTRUCTION — only about how playing time was distributed among the cards that played.

VALIDATION OF THE RUN-DEPTH IDENTITY (team games = ΣGS_1). Exactly one pitcher starts each game, so
ΣGS_1 should equal team games, and max(G, G_1) over the roster is a LOWER BOUND on it (some player
usually, but not always, appears in every game). The identity therefore predicts ΣGS_1 ≥ max(G,G_1)
with NO exceptions, and equality whenever any one card played every game. Measured:
  ΣGS_1 ≥ max(G,G_1): 1595/1595 teams (100.0%) — ZERO violations, as predicted.
  ΣGS_1 = max(G,G_1): 1377/1595 (86.3%); where it exceeds, the gap is median 1, p99 4,
  and it GROWS with run length as rest days predict — mean gap 0.01 at ≤7 games, 0.90 at 15+.
  A missing-starter-row defect would produce ΣGS_1 BELOW max(G,G_1); none occurs anywhere. (max must
  NOT include G_2: the fielding table is per-POSITION, so a card that changes position mid-game
  books two fielding games — which is why the naive three-way max disagrees 61% of the time.)

§2.  QUESTION 1 — THE HITTER PLAYING-TIME DISTRIBUTION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DEFINITIONS. A row is a pitcher-row iff BF>0. HITTER PA = PA on non-pitcher rows, so pitcher
batting (real in the no-DH Early Gold) is excluded and reported separately. Per team the hitter PA
vector is sorted descending; rank k = 'the k-th most-used hitter' — the same ordering the model's
curve uses (lineupWraa sorts wOBA desc against lineupPA). N_eff = 1 / Σ sᵢ² on those shares: the
number of EQUALLY-used hitters producing the same concentration. Nine everyday players and a dead
bench scores exactly 9.0. 'Full-time share' = 1/L of team hitter PA, L = the format's lineup size.

MODEL BASELINE   lineupPA (1−0.03·slot):  9 slots → N_eff 8.931   8 slots → N_eff 7.953
                 9-slot shares: 0.1263  0.1225  0.1187  0.1149  0.1111  0.1073  0.1035  0.0997  0.0960
                 slot-1 : slot-9 = 1.316
                 The bench enters ONLY via fullStrengthShare (offense.ts): at f0=0.6 each starter
                 plays 0.9556 of games and ALL substitutes together take 4.44% of lineup PA.
                 Spreading that allowance evenly over the whole bench — the most generous possible
                 reading of the model — the implied roster-wide N_eff still only reaches:
                    bench of  4 → N_eff 9.734
                    bench of  8 → N_eff 9.757
                    bench of 13 → N_eff 9.766
                    bench of 17 → N_eff 9.770
                 So ~9.77 is the model's CEILING on hitter spread. Everything above it is a miss.

── 3b. bullpen leverage — is a two-number model enough? ─────────────────────

`bullpenLeverage [2.5,1.5]` reweights bullpen BF: top arm 2.5× a filler arm, 2nd 1.5×, everyone
from the 3rd down EXACTLY 1.0×. Two separable claims:
  (i)  the LEVEL of the slot-1/slot-2 premium;
  (ii) the FLAT-FROM-SLOT-3 claim — which is the actual content of 'a two-number model'.

Relievers = pure relief rows (GS_1=0, G_1>0). THE ORDERING IS ITSELF A MODELLING CHOICE and one
candidate is circular, so all three are shown:
  by BF   ranks arms by the very quantity measured. Mechanically maximal concentration — an UPPER
          BOUND on how peaked any leverage rule could look. NOT the curve.
  by pLi  ranks by realised average LEVERAGE INDEX, i.e. by ROLE (closer / setup / mop-up). This is
          what bullpenLeverage means, and it is not circular in BF.  ← the comparison that counts
  BF×pLi  leverage-weighted innings — the quantity the code comment says it models.

VERDICT — AND THE PARAMETER TURNS OUT TO BE AMBIGUOUS, WHICH IS THE REAL FINDING.

`bullpenLeverage` multiplies `bullpenBF`, and `bullpenBF` is consumed in defenseRunsAboveAvg as a
straight BATTERS-FACED weight. But the code comment calls it 'leverage-weighted bullpen BF'. Those
are two different quantities and the data answers them OPPOSITELY, so the parameter cannot be
right under both readings and it is not clear which one it is meant to be.

  READING A — plain BF, i.e. 'the closer FACES MORE BATTERS than the 7th man'.
     realised (role order, ≥7-arm neutral teams):  0.138  0.138  0.140  0.145  0.141  0.141  0.123
     model:                                        0.278  0.167  0.111  0.111  0.111  0.111  0.111
     Realised bullpen BF is FLAT across leverage roles — RP1/RP7 = 1.12, against the model's 2.50.
     The high-leverage arm does NOT throw more; it throws the same amount in tighter spots. Under
     this reading the FLAT-TAIL claim is right and the PREMIUM is refuted — the model over-
     concentrates the top slot by 2.0×.

  READING B — leverage-weighted innings (BF × pLi), what the comment says.
     realised (≥7-arm neutral teams):              0.322  0.211  0.154  0.118  0.087  0.062  0.037

  underlying driver is visible with no BF circularity at all: the leverage index itself falls
  monotonically 2.43 → 0.43 from closer to 7th man, with no flat region anywhere. A
  two-number [premium, premium, flat…] shape cannot represent a smooth monotone decline no matter
  what the two numbers are. NOTE the ordering matters enormously and the intuitive one is wrong:
  ranking arms by their own BF gives RP1/RP7 = 3.46, but that ranks arms by the very quantity
  being measured and is circular — it is an upper bound on peakedness, not an estimate of it.

§4.  QUESTION 3 — fullStrengthShare AND platoonCapture
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

── 4a. platoonCapture — THE EXPORTS CANNOT ADDRESS IT ───────────────────────

ρ is 'how often a FIELDED CARD gets its favourable matchup'. Identifying it needs each hitter's PA
split by opposing-pitcher handedness. The export carries vL/vR columns for RATINGS only (BA vL,
POW vR, STU vL, …); every realised statistic — PA, AB, H, HR, BB, K, IP, BF — is a single
undifferentiated total. No PA-vs-LHP column, no split table, no game log. ρ is NOT MEASURABLE from
this corpus and NO PROXY IS OFFERED.

What IS measurable is the handedness SUPPLY — the exposure under zero platoon deployment:

  group                      BF by RHP   BF by LHP   LHP share
  Quicks - Bronze                26944       14816       35.5%
  Quicks - Gold                  22817       14235       38.4%
  Quicks - Open                  16797       10200       37.8%
  Return of the Bronze          104958       59057       36.0%
  Early Gold                    190085       76925       28.8%
  POOL: 4 neutral DH fmts       171516       98308       36.4%

  In the neutral pool 36.4% of BF come from LHP, so a randomly-deployed LH hitter meets its
  favourable side 63.6% of the time and a RH hitter 36.4%. These are SUPPLY numbers and they
  bound nothing about ρ, because ρ is the manager's deployment ON TOP of supply — exactly what the
  export does not record. Quoted only so nobody mistakes supply for capture.

── 4b. fullStrengthShare — its SHAPE is testable; its VALUE is not ──────────

f0 = 'fraction of games fielded at the best nine AVAILABLE'. Availability (injury, fatigue) is not
exported and 'best' is a model judgement, so f0 is not identified. But it has ONE implication that
does live in the data: in offense.ts, with probability (1−f0) exactly ONE of the L starters is
replaced, so the L starters must collectively take (L−1+f0)/L of all lineup starts. GS (batting
games started) is exported, so that share is directly measurable.

CONFOUND, NAMED: the measured churn conflates unavailability with deliberate platooning, rest,
blowout substitution and manager preference — the model attributes ALL of it to f0. So this is an
implication test of the RULE'S SHAPE, not an estimate of f0.

SCOPE NOTES, KEPT AT THE END BUT NOT BURIED — every one of them constrains the table above.
  1. OWNERSHIP SHAPING. These rosters are built from cards people own; composition reflects
     collections and the market. Nothing here supports a claim about what a roster COULD be. Every
     claim is about how playing time distributes ACROSS A ROSTER THAT EXISTS — which is what the
     usage model consumes, so the shaping does not touch the estimand.
  2. NO CAP, NO SLOTS. All five formats are budget_mode:none. The cap/slots objective is the main
     consumer of these parameters and the corpus cannot speak to it.
  3. ONE ENV-MODIFIED FORMAT. Early Gold's divergence cannot be attributed between environment,
     no-DH, min_starters 4 and era-1920 pool composition from n=1 format. §3's stamina measurement
     points at pool composition; that is a lead, not a result.
  4. DESCRIPTIVE ONLY. No curve was fitted, no parameter proposed, no default touched. A
