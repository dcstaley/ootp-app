## 4. RATING SHAPE — is the paired opposing rating enough, or is the whole profile moving?
################################################################################

     The doctrine is that a card's quality is never inferrable from ONE rating. If an opponent
     term is built as "residual ~ the single paired opposing rating", that assumption is being
     made implicitly. Test it on the design points the composition actually supplies: the
     7 seasons × 4 matchup cells = 28 distinct opposing-profile vectors.

     correlation matrix of the opposing HITTER profile across the 28 (season × cell) design points:

     READ, with the caveat stated first: 28 design points against 4–5 predictors is thin, and
     LOO on 28 points is itself noisy — treat this as a DIRECTIONAL warning, not a measurement.
     Directionally it is one-sided: the full opposing PROFILE beats the single paired rating on
     7 of 8 channels, and for PITCHER hits the best single predictor of the residual is the
     opposing kRat — NOT its own pair (babip). The opposing ratings are not independent
     (mean |r| 0.45 on the hitter side, 0.35 on the pitcher side), so "the paired rating" is
     partly standing in for a correlated bundle. DESIGN CONSTRAINT: an opponent term should be
     specified over the opposing card's PROFILE, not its single paired rating — consistent with
     the rating-shape doctrine. A single-rating pairing is a modelling assumption this data
     does not support, and it is cheapest to avoid making it now.

## 5. WHAT THIS IMPLIES — per-channel or model-wide?
################################################################################

  MODEL-WIDE, NOT PER-CHANNEL — but the payoff is concentrated. The hole is structural: no
  channel in either role has an opponent term, and the four count channels that carry the
  wOBA weight (K and BB in both roles) are exactly the four where composition is largest:
     PITCHER BB 62.6%,  PITCHER K 53.8%,  HITTER BB 38.9%,  HITTER K 31.0%
  Those four alone account for 40.6 of the 43.0 total mean-square the composition removes
  across all nine channels — 94.5% of the whole prize. Building the term for K and BB in both
  roles captures nearly all of it; the power and BIP channels are follow-on, not motivation.

  MATERIAL BUT SECONDARY: HITTER hits(nonHR) 14.1% (resid sd only 3.31/600); HITTER HR 11.5% (resid sd only 1.23/600); PITCHER HR 9.6% (resid sd only 1.19/600).
  ROUNDING ERROR: PITCHER hits(nonHR) 3.2%; HITTER XBH 2.2%.

  THE PRICING RATIOS SAY THE TERM IS ALREADY SPECIFIED, NOT INVENTED. Across the four big
  channels the residual moves with the opposing rating at pitcher BB 0.76×, pitcher K 1.14×, hitter BB 0.60×, hitter K 1.30×
  what the model's own complementary curve already prices that rating point at (1.00 = exact).
  The model contains the number; it
  simply never applies it to the opponent. That is an argument for a SYMMETRIC term derived
  from the existing curves rather than a new free-parameter block.

  WHERE THE EVIDENCE IS TOO THIN TO SAY — flagged explicitly:
   • PITCHER hits(nonHR): composition share 3.2%, level gap +0.19/600 covering 0 (p=0.558),
     sign consistency only 18/33, and a pricing ratio of -0.16× whose CI spans zero and the wrong
     sign. This channel shows NO usable opponent signal on the paired rating. Do not build one
     for it on this evidence.
   • HITTER hits(nonHR): the composition GAP is +0.19 rating points — essentially nothing — yet
     the fitted response is 3.93× the model's own pricing. A slope that large off a gap that
     small is identified by season drift, not by the matchup, so it is not evidence for a
     matchup opponent term. Treat the 14.1% share as an upper bound.
   • HITTER XBH: unpairable. There is no pitcher GAP rating; predictPitching hard-codes the
     0.25 XBH share. Any opponent term here needs a rating the card data does not carry.
   • Both HR channels: shares of ~10–12% but on residual sds of ~1.2/600. Real, tiny, and the
     18/33 and 23/33 league-season sign counts are not the near-unanimity the K and BB channels show.

  A CAVEAT THAT APPLIES TO EVERY NUMBER ABOVE. Same-side exposure is MANAGER-SELECTED — a
  pitcher gets platoon-advantage batters because someone chose the matchup. For PREDICTION
  that distinction does not matter (the term models who was actually faced), but the SIZE of
  the effect is a property of this corpus's usage patterns. Tournament rosters and usage
  differ, so the coefficient should be re-measured there before it is trusted out of frame.
  And this survey does not establish the FORM of an opponent term — only that the hole is
  real, where it is largest, and that the model already contains the pricing it would need.
