4. COMPANION — LEVEL RESIDUALS OF THE HEAVILY-PLAYED LOW-K CARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Performance evidence only.

   Among the 68 heavily-played cards, tertiles by observed K9:
     low-K   n=23  med BF 2725  resid K9 +0.58  resid wOBAA +0.0121  obs RA9 4.10
     mid     n=22  med BF 4079  resid K9 -0.09  resid wOBAA +0.0193  obs RA9 3.86
     high-K  n=23  med BF 2000  resid K9 -0.52  resid wOBAA +0.0190  obs RA9 3.77
   (identical picture when the tertile is cut on PREDICTED K9 instead.)

   THREE READINGS, IN ORDER OF WHAT THEY SUPPORT:
   (a) REVEALED PREFERENCE IS REAL AND RUNS TOWARD LOW-K. Gold's field plays the
       low-K tertile at a HIGHER median BF than the high-K tertile (2725 vs 2000;
       3265 vs 1981 on the predicted cut). The most-played cards in the tier are
       low-K: Dock Ellis 37,162 BF, Old Hoss Radbourn 13,836, Mark Fidrych 11,881.
       Whatever the field is selecting on, it is not strikeouts.
   (b) THE SPREAD DEFICIT IS VISIBLE INSIDE THE HEAVY SET ALONE. resid K9 runs
       +0.58 / -0.09 / -0.52 low->high. That is the need (a slope > 1) expressed as
       a level residual, and it is present at BF>=1000 with the light cards gone. So
       the bar does not make gold's K problem go away; it makes it smaller.
   (c) THE OUTPERFORMANCE IS NOT LOW-K-SPECIFIC — SO IT IS NOT A MISVALUATION SIGNAL
       FOR THIS COHORT. Every tertile allows LESS wOBA than predicted (+0.0121 to
       +0.0193), i.e. the level offset is a whole-population property of the gold
       cell, and the low-K tertile outperforms the LEAST of the three. It also
       allows the most runs in absolute terms (RA9 4.10 vs 3.77). The heavily-played
       low-K cards are therefore NOT hidden value the model is missing; the field
       prefers them for something the K channel does not price, and they do not beat
       their own prediction by more than anyone else. Individually the pattern holds
       (Mark Fidrych resid K9 +1.40 / wOBAA +0.0223; Warren Spahn +1.54 / +0.0043;
       Tony Watson +0.32 / -0.0112 — one of only two negatives in the top 12).

5. WHICH WORLD — AND WHY NOT THE OTHER TWO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   WEAK-TAIL-DOMINATED. The support-leverage story stands and 1.63 is the cleaner
   number. The counts and variance shares that decide it:
     · the entire 1.78 -> 1.63 move is reproduced by removing 5 cards, all of which
       sit below the league training 5th percentile in effective stuff, at training
       percentile <= 0.1%, carrying 31.5% of gold's predicted-K variance;
     · removing the 18 lightly-used SUPPORTED cards instead moves the slope +0.01;
     · the release channel isolated from the support channel moves it +0.02.

   NEW-RELEASE-DOMINATED IS REFUTED, on its mechanism rather than by a p-value:
   the world requires short-lined new elite high-K cards. There are none. Zero July
   and zero in-window cards in the top predicted-K decile; three in-window cards in
   the whole 91, two of which already clear BF>=1000; 70% of the drop-out set
   released before June. It follows that GOLD'S NEED IS NOT ACTIVELY DRIFTING for
   this reason and a re-pull in 1-2 weeks is NOT the confirmation to reach for — it
   would move these numbers only by adding play to cards that already have it.

   MIXED IS NOT SUPPORTED. Reporting both tags separately is exactly what §2b does,
   and one of the two comes back at +0.02. There is nothing to average away.

   WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS. The verdict attributes a 0.15 slope move to five named,
   individually listed cards. That is a large, reproducible variance share but a
   small number of cards, and the cell they occupy is THIN by the standing rule. The
   attribution would be overturned if those five cards' observed K9 moved materially
   on a later pull, or if a widened sample (more formats at the same value window)
   put a different set of cards in the same coordinate and did not reproduce it.
   Nothing here fits, proposes, or wires a correction for any of it.

   ONE FINDING THE BAR DOES NOT FIX, FOR THE RECORD. BF>=1000 removes 5 of the 11
   sub-p05 cards; the other 6 stay, carrying 19.3% of the variance. Removing ALL
   eleven takes gold to 1.53. So the support-leverage problem is attenuated by the
   usage floor, not solved by it — which is a statement about the K curve's low-end
   support, not about the bar.
