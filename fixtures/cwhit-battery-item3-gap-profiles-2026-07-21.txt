# READING (descriptive only — no coordinate proposed, no form implied)

GAP LEVEL DOES NOT DECOUPLE AT GOLD. Every channel, both roles, falls monotonically
iron -> diamond. Gold sits between silver and diamond everywhere, on both sides. So the
question item 3 was posed to answer — "does gold's hitter-side vs pitcher-side off-frame
profile decouple where other tiers' don't?" — answers NO in gap LEVEL. Gold is ordinary
on the axis the ramp currently reads.

DISPERSION IS WHERE GOLD IS UNUSUAL. Within the top-50 field, gold is a LOCAL EXTREMUM
(relative to silver and diamond) on 6 of the 9 channels:
    LOCAL MIN  hit.eye 33.67 · hit.pow 26.94 · hit.kRat 31.87 · pit.stu 26.26
    LOCAL MAX  hit.gap 28.30 · pit.pbabip 33.45
  Only pit.con is monotone across all five tiers; hit.babip and pit.hrr are non-monotone
  but with gold on a limb rather than at a turn.

THE CONTRAST IS THE POINT. Gold is unremarkable in the FIRST moment (gap) and a turning
point in the SECOND (dispersion) on two thirds of channels. The K need that no gap-only
ramp can fit — gold 1.78 at gap 15.9, above silver's 1.48 at gap 20.5 — is a need the
first moment cannot see.

SPECIFICALLY ON THE K COORDINATE. Pitcher K is scored on the CROSSED gap, i.e. the
opposing hitters' kRat gap, not the pitchers' own stu gap. Both are monotone. But
hit.kRat DISPERSION is 36.64 at silver and 31.87 at gold — gold's opposing-K field is
TIGHTER than silver's while its gap is smaller. Two tiers whose opponents differ in
spread, scored on a coordinate that records only where the opponents sit on average.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. This is one pool per tier, five nested pools, nine channels — 6-of-9 is
a pattern worth carrying, NOT a result. It is descriptive support for looking beyond the
mean gap; it does not identify a coordinate, does not rank candidates, and cannot on its
own distinguish a real second-moment dependence from nesting artefacts. Items 1, 4 and 5
are what would settle that.
