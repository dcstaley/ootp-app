THE THREE NUMBERS THAT MATTER, all measured, none assumed:

  train leg, pitchers, USAGE-WEIGHTED variant share ......  2.5%   (11.0% hitters)
  tournament REALIZED variant share of usage (item 4) ....  15-29%
  arm-C pool FIELD variant share (top-50, measured) ......  84-96%   (42-48 of 50)

Derek's claim is confirmed in direction and is larger than expected: the league genuinely
carries FEW variants — 2.5% of qualifying pitcher usage — because league play runs on new
100+ cards whose variants are rare. Tournaments genuinely carry MORE (15-29%). Both are
EMPIRICAL. The third number is not: it is a construction choice, because arm C manufactures
a v5 for every eligible card and the boost then wins nearly every top-50 seat.

SO ARM C DOES NOT SATISFY THE INVARIANT — IT INVERTS IT.
  · today (arm A):  train 2.5% variant  vs  pool 0% variant     → mismatch ~2.5 points
  · arm C as ruled: train 2.5% variant  vs  pool ~88% variant   → mismatch ~85 points
The invariant was stated as "same variant policy on both sides", and arm C does apply the
same policy NAME to both legs. But the legs draw on sources with wildly different variant
availability, so an identical policy produces wildly different populations. Comparability is
the thing the invariant exists to protect; the policy label is only a proxy for it, and here
the proxy and the goal point in opposite directions.

WHY THE PROXY FAILS HERE, PRECISELY. The train leg is EMPIRICAL — it is whatever the league
actually played, variants included (`includeVariants: true`), and the league's answer happens
to be "hardly any". The pool leg under arm C is THEORETICAL — every eligible card is given a
v5 whether or not one exists, can exist (36.9% cannot at all), or is owned by anyone. One leg
reports reality; the other reports a hypothesis. Making the hypothesis "maximally inclusive"
moves it further from the reality it is differenced against.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT OVERTURN. The DEFECT arm C was ruled to fix is real: today's legs are
mismatched (2.5% vs 0%) and mismatched in the same direction the audit found on two other
axes. The finding is that arm C is the wrong REPAIR, not that there is nothing to repair.
Note also the sizes: this 2.5-point mismatch is far smaller than the one arm C would create,
which reframes the whole variant axis as the smallest of the three gap defects rather than
the largest — the opposite of what item A's sizing (deviation 100%, "in a class of its own")
implied, because that sizing measured arm C's effect rather than the defect's.

THE REPAIR THIS EVIDENCE POINTS AT, stated as an option and not a recommendation: match the
pool leg to the tournament's OWN realized variant presence (15-29%) rather than to 0% or to
100%. That is a FIELD estimating the population actually met — Fable's own baseline-vs-field
definition — and both legs would then be empirical. It needs the eligibility predicate
(36.9% of cards cannot have a v5) and a presence rule, both of which are open.

(end of artifact — arm C as ruled inverts the invariant it was meant to satisfy)
