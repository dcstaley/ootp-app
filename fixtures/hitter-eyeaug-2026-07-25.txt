strikeouts, the direction the finding reports for high-Eye hitters). Slopes are PA-weighted, per
SD of raw EYE, matching the published +2.01 marginal / +2.15 conditional exactly in units and
grain. CONDITIONAL holds all five hitter ratings (eye, pow, kRat, babip, gap) — the decisive read.

READ THE TWO FRAMES DIFFERENTLY, and this is the whole methodological point:
  · IN FRAME the aux is fitted by weighted least squares against ln(EYE), so the marginal
    ln(EYE) direction of the K residual is SUPPRESSED BY CONSTRUCTION. A near-zero in-frame
    slope is therefore only a sanity check that the term is the right FUNCTIONAL FORM (the aux

    is not forced to zero at all, so neither reading is a tautology — but neither is evidence).

THE LEVERAGE ARITHMETIC — why a large K fix is a small wOBA fix. A strikeout carries NO wOBA
weight; it moves wOBA ONLY by removing a ball in play, and the fitted ln(BIP) elasticity is
BELOW 1, so only a fraction of a recovered BIP becomes a hit (§7 measures the pass-through
directly). Scale, on the 2042+2043 fit rows:
  sd of the prediction CHANGE the aux causes:  0.00097 wOBA
  sd of ACTUAL wOBA across the same cards:     0.01800 wOBA
  ⇒ the aux repositions cards by 5.4% of the card-to-card spread the metric ranks on.
  A Pearson shift of the size §5 could resolve (~±0.003 at these n) is simply not available
  from a term with that lever, however right the term is about strikeouts.

pattern is a coin flip on numerical noise, not a weak positive trend, and it must not be
reported as "directionally favourable". The correct statement is that ΔwPearson is ZERO.

PINNING: the aux CANNOT change which channels pin on this form. hit.hr is the only quad and it
is fitted before the BIP chain exists, on (POW, HR, w) alone; every other hitter channel is LOG
and pinEvent requires a rawpoly-2. §3 confirms it empirically on all three windows. Unlike the
stuffAug arm — where the aux moved pit.hr's vertex in-domain on [2042,2043] and cost that
comparison its one-term status — THIS IS A STRICT ONE-TERM CONTRAST.

BIP KNOCK-ON: reported in §7 and not negligible. The aux moves predicted K in OPPOSITE
directions for high-Eye and low-Eye cards, so the pooled mean barely moves while individual
cards move materially; each strikeout the model stops predicting becomes a ball in play and
roughly the fitted BABIP fraction of it becomes a hit, which travels into predicted wOBA. The
candidate REFITS hit.h on the new predicted-BIP column, so most of the mechanical effect is
re-absorbed by the h curve rather than propagating raw.

WHY BOTH RESULTS ARE TRUE AT ONCE — the part that matters more than the verdict. A strikeout
carries NO wOBA weight in this form: it moves wOBA ONLY by removing a ball in play, and the
fitted ln(BIP) elasticity is below 1, so only about a fifth of a recovered strikeout becomes a
hit (§7's measured pass-through). The aux moves individual cards by up to ~7 K/600 — a large,
real, correctly-signed correction — and that lands as a prediction change whose spread is a
few percent of the card-to-card wOBA spread the metric ranks on (§8). A term with that lever
CANNOT produce a resolvable Pearson gain at this n no matter how right it is. The residual
finding and this null are therefore not in conflict, and neither one overturns the other:
the deployed model IS mis-predicting strikeouts on the EYE axis, and fixing that does not
measurably improve the wOBA ranking it is used for.

AND A REASON THE TERM MAY BE ACTIVELY UNWANTED — the strongest argument against it, and it is
NOT the null. The residual artifact found the composite EYE slope sits at ~0 BY CANCELLATION
between oppositely-signed channels (K −0.000033, BABIP +0.000025, HR +0.000019). Removing the
K leg does not have to help the composite — it can UNBALANCE it. Measured in §8, it does:
  IN FRAME   fit 2042+2043 → measure 2042+2043 -0.00003  → +0.00081 
  OUT FRAME  fit 2042+2043 → measure 2037–2041 -0.00100★ → -0.00022 
  OUT FRAME  fit 2037+2038 → measure 2039–2043 +0.00036  → +0.00129★
  OUT FRAME  fit 2040+2041 → measure 2042+2043 +0.00112★ → +0.00209★
  (conditional composite wOBA bias per SD EYE; ★ = CI excludes zero.)
  ⇒ |slope| GROWS in 3 of 4 panels. In 1 the candidate's composite EYE bias is CI-CLEAR where
  the deployed form's was NOT; in 1 the reverse. So on the quantity the model is actually
  used for, correcting the strikeout channel makes the EYE-axis bias LARGER more often than
  smaller. The channel fix is real and the composite consequence is adverse — which is exactly
  what a cancellation predicts, and exactly why a channel-level residual is not by itself a
  mandate to add a term. (Magnitudes are ≤0.002 wOBA per SD throughout, small in absolute
  terms; the point is the DIRECTION and that it is the opposite of the intended improvement.)

RECOMMENDATION: DO NOT ADOPT, and do NOT discard the finding. The candidate stays in
FORM_ENTRIES as a scored candidate (nothing deployed, no default changed) so it is re-measured
on every future scoreboard run. The reason to keep it on the books rather than delete it: the
term's coefficient is stable across all three windows (−2.12 to −2.59 K/600 per SD of raw EYE)
and matches the independently-measured residual slope (+2.01 to +2.15) almost exactly — that
is what a real channel looks like, and the sequential-resolution mechanism is the same one
that carried stuffAug. What is missing is the LEVER, not the effect.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER, and neither is available in league data today: (i) a deliverable
metric sensitive to strikeouts in their own right rather than only through wOBA volume; (ii) an
OUT-OF-FRAME pool whose EYE distribution is shifted far enough that a +2/600 K error stops
being a wOBA rounding error — the tournament ladder, not the league. NOTE that (ii) cuts both
ways: the same shift that would make the fix pay would also amplify the composite unbalancing
measured above, so the ladder test must read the COMPOSITE, not just the K channel.

A METHOD POINT WORTH KEEPING. This is the first candidate in the program that removed its
motivating residual almost completely and still failed. That combination is only visible
because the residual re-measurement (§6) and the metric comparison (§5) were run as SEPARATE
questions. Had only §6 been run the term would look like a clear win; had only §5 been run it
would look like an irrelevant null. A channel-level residual is evidence that the FORM is
wrong; it is not evidence that correcting it improves the thing the model is used for.

DATA LIMITS, stated so the deliverable metrics are not over-read: only a few dozen distinct
CARDS clear the 1000-PA bar per test block, so the top-26 intervals in §5 run to roughly ±0.08
to ±0.12 — two to three roster slots out of 26. Ordinal rankings in §4 are NOT evidence.
League coverage is unequal by season (2037: 4 leagues; 2039 effectively 4; the rest 5).
