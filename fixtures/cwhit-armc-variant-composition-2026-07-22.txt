# READING — one correction to the record, one constraint on the ramp fit
################################################################################

1. THE ARM-C FIELD IS DOMINANTLY THE SAME CARDS, BOOSTED. 42-48 of every top-50 are v5
   rows. It is not 50/50 because a strong base card can outrank a weaker card's v5, but the
   selection is overwhelmingly "each good card's variant". Since variantBoost is
   deterministic and strictly positive (+5 at rating 40 rising to +13 at 180), the
   variant-inclusive pool is the base pool plus a KNOWN image of itself. Arm C admits no
   independent information; it re-bases the field.

   This is what item A saw and did not name: ordering preserved, inversions 2->2, Spearman
   0.700 unchanged, residual signs flipping from mixed to UNIFORMLY NEGATIVE. Those are
   level-shift signatures, and now there is a mechanism for them.

2. CORRECTION TO THE RECORD — DIAMOND'S "IN-FRAME" GAIN IS NOT INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION.
   I previously relayed it as a second argument for arm C alongside the invariant. It is
   not: the mean stuff boost over each pool's base top-50 is +6.8 to +7.9, and diamond's
   gap was 10.38. A gap of ~10 minus a re-basing of ~8-10 lands near zero by ARITHMETIC.
   Diamond's measured need is 1.04 [0.80, 1.27], so s = 1 is consistent with it — but
   landing at gap -0.04 is the boost magnitude happening to approximate diamond's gap, not
   the model discovering that diamond is in-frame. THE CASE FOR ARM C RESTS ON THE
   INVARIANT (both legs of a gap on the same basis), which is what it was ruled on.

3. BUT THE BOOST IS NOT THE WHOLE STORY — there IS a selection component, and it is not
   uniform. If arm C were purely a re-basing, every gap would fall by about the boost
   (+6.8..+7.9, a tight range). Item A measured gap deltas of -4.64 (iron), -10.47
   (bronze), -8.55 (silver), -9.16 (gold), -10.42 (diamond) — a much wider spread, with
   iron moving LESS than its boost and bronze/diamond moving MORE. So the deterministic
   re-basing is the dominant term and a genuine per-tier re-selection of ~±3 rating points
   rides on top of it. Both are real; neither alone explains the deltas.

4. THE CONSTRAINT THIS PUTS ON C3. Under arm C the gap axis COMPRESSES: 19.00 / 12.29 /
   12.00 / 6.79 / -0.04 against today's 23.64 / 22.76 / 20.55 / 15.95 / 10.38. The coherent
   tiers now span roughly 12-19 instead of 20-24, and diamond sits at zero where the ramp is
   the identity by construction. So the re-estimated ramp is fit over a NARROWER gap range
   with one fewer informative point. G was already only LOWER-BOUNDED in the original fit
   (SSE flat within 5% across a wide G range, which is why the pinning rule exists); on a
   compressed axis it will be less identifiable still. C3's pre-registration should say in
   advance how G is pinned under the new axis and must treat a boundary-pinned optimum as
   family misfit, per the standing rule.
