================================================================================
VERDICT — is minPA=1000 costing us, does a wider window help once drift is absorbed,
and does either change the EYE-axis verdict?
================================================================================

SHORT ANSWER: minPA=1000 is costing us almost exactly nothing, a wider window costs us a little,
and neither rescues the EYE axis. The day's nulls were not sample-size nulls.

1. IS minPA=1000 COSTING US?  Barely — and the honest answer has two halves.
   Under R1 (out-of-time ΔwPearson over six numbers, test set FIXED at the deployed bar so this is
   a power question and not a population shift), thresholds 250, 500, 750 clear the rule; the best is
   minPA=250 at mean ΔwPearson +0.0009 (5/6 positive, 1 CI-clear positive, 0 CI-clear negative), and
   minPA=500 is the sign-consistent one at 6/6 positive. So the sweep does have an interior optimum
   and 1000 is not it.
   BUT THE SIZE IS THE POINT. The gain is +0.0006 to +0.0009 in weighted Pearson. The comparator
   this project adopts on is stuffAug at +0.012…+0.033 — one to two ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE larger. On
   the deliverable metrics the gain is not there at all: Δtop26 and Δregret are ~0 at every
   threshold, against top-26 intervals of ±0.08-0.19. Moving the bar to 500 would change no roster.
   AND THE OTHER DIRECTION IS REAL. Dropping the bar to zero is clearly BAD: mean ΔwPearson
   -0.0031 with the pitcher in-window fit collapsing (-0.025) and pitcher CV collapsing (-0.063).
   So there is a genuine floor: the low-PA rows are not free information, they are noise the
   PA^0.75 weight only partly suppresses. That is the population caveat in numbers — "more cards"
   and "more information" are different quantities, and past ~250 PA the extra cards stop being
   information and start being noise.
   AND RELAXING DOES NOT NARROW ANYTHING (§1d). If the bar comes down on BOTH sides — the only way
   an interval could narrow — it WIDENS: hitter wPearson half-width ±0.0260 at 1000 →
   ±0.0489 at no bar; top-26 stays ±0.13-0.19 throughout. The data-limited deliverable metrics
   are NOT limited by the threshold. That closes the "just lower the bar and the CIs will tighten"
   hope directly, and it is the single most transferable result in this artifact.

2. DOES A WIDER WINDOW HELP ONCE DRIFT IS ABSORBED?  No — and the drift absorption is a null with
   a mechanism, which is more useful than a null without one.
   Under R2 (test year PINNED, window grown away from it, so every width is scored on byte-identical
   test rows), NO eligible width clears the rule. Widening is neutral for pitchers and mildly NEGATIVE for
   hitters — the forward design puts k=3,4,5 CI-clear below k=2 (-0.0033, -0.0036, -0.0044). Only
   at k=6 does it climb back to roughly even, and by then the oldest season is six years from the
   test year.
   THE ABSORBED ARM MOVED ALMOST NOTHING (§2d), and the reason is structural rather than
   disappointing: this trainer POOLS a card's counts across the window's seasons, so a per-season
   level shift reaches a card only through that card's season MIX. Cards with similar mixes all move
   together, and a common shift is exactly what the fit's intercept already absorbs. Measured, the
   differential part is ~1-4% of the between-card spread on every channel. So the season fixed
   effect is not wrong — it has nothing left to remove once the seasons are pooled. The wide-window
   measurement's finding still stands (LEVEL drifts, SHAPE does not); what this run adds is that the
   drift was never entering the pooled fit as anything but an intercept.
   The 7-season window cannot be judged on the metric that decides at all — it leaves no held-out
   block (§2c). Its CV numbers are shown and are NOT evidence.

3. DOES EITHER CHANGE THE EYE-AXIS VERDICT?  No. It is confirmed, and one of the two legs is
   confirmed in the opposite direction from the "power failure" hypothesis.
   THE THRESHOLD MAKES THE BABIP AUX WORSE, NOT BETTER. Its swing across the three published fit
   windows goes 4.16x (minPA 1000) → 5.81x (500) → 8.11x (250) → 11.94x (no bar).
   Adding the low-PA cards DESTABILISES exactly the leg that was unstable. That is the cleanest
   result in the artifact: under-determination here is not a shortage of rows, it is a shortage of
   INFORMATION about the contact channel, and low-PA rows carry the least of it per row.
   AND THE SIGNS DO NOT STOP SORTING BY FIT WINDOW. 3 of the 5 arms sort perfectly at minPA 1000;
   at minPA 250 it is 4 of 5 — relaxing the bar made MORE arms sort, not fewer (§3c). At the best
   (threshold, window) that R1 and R2 actually selected — minPA 250, 2 seasons — R3 needed BOTH the
   coefficients to steady AND the sort to break. Neither happened, so the verdict is unchanged.
   THE ONE PLACE IT LOOKS DIFFERENT, HANDLED HONESTLY. At WIDTH 3 (which did NOT win R2) the sign
   sort does break — but into SCATTER, not into consistency: the arms still land 3-4 of 6 positive
   with a -0.0035 in one panel, so the adoption bar (consistently positive, never CI-clear negative)
   is still missed by the same distance. And the coefficient story there does not hold up either.
   On the MATCHED two-window basis (Table D) widening steadies the BABIP aux at minPA 250
   (1.77x → 1.24x) and DESTABILISES it at minPA 1000 (1.06x → 1.58x). A parameter whose
   apparent identification flips direction with a nuisance knob is not identified; the swing factor
   is itself unstable. The K aux meanwhile sits at 1.01-1.14 in every cell of every table — which is
   what an identified term looks like, and it is the leg that was never in doubt.
   Drift absorption changes the coefficients by ~0.04x, i.e. not at all.
   ⇒ THE EYE-AXIS DECLINE STANDS, and it now stands on evidence rather than on assumption. The
   four-leg candidate was not a victim of the 74-card sample; it is not identified by this league's
   data at any threshold or width available, and the six-leg cancellation finding (removing four legs
   of a six-leg cancellation lands wherever the other two sit) remains the reason it cannot pay.

WHAT THIS CLOSES. "It failed because we only had ~75 cards" is no longer available as an
explanation for today's nulls. The 75 is a threshold artefact — the window really does hold 162
hitters and 155 pitchers — but the discarded rows carry so little estimating weight (PA^0.75 already
gives a 250-PA card ~35% of a 1000-PA card's) and so much noise that admitting them buys ~0.0006
Pearson and costs interval width. Two years and 1000 PA are, to within the resolution of every
metric we act on, the right settings. NO PRODUCTION DEFAULT SHOULD CHANGE ON THIS ARTIFACT.

ONE THING IT DOES NOT CLOSE (§5): the per-event flex ceiling spends 21/15 parameters against these
same row counts. A 5-parameter curve being unable to use extra rows does not prove a 21-parameter
one cannot — flexible forms have more to gain. What it does establish is that the extra rows are
available and are LOW QUALITY, so the ceiling's parameter-cost problem cannot be fixed by lowering
the bar alone. The ceiling's own sweep is a separate measurement and was not run here.

PRE-REGISTERED DECISION RULES — fixed before the results below were read
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The metric that decides is OUT-OF-TIME ΔwPearson, never in-sample and never CV alone: a lower
threshold trivially improves in-sample coverage, and CV shares the window's frame. In-sample and
CV are reported as the overfit diagnostic (a term that buys in-sample fit and gives it back out of
time is the signature this run is looking for).

R1 THRESHOLD. A threshold beats minPA=1000 only if, across the THREE out-of-time panels × TWO
   roles (six numbers, test set fixed at the deployed bar): the mean ΔwPearson vs minPA=1000 is
   POSITIVE, at least 4 of 6 individual signs are positive, and NONE is CI-clear negative.
   Otherwise minPA=1000 stands. Ties go to the incumbent.
R2 WINDOW. A width beats 2 seasons only if, across the TWO out-of-time designs × TWO roles (four
   numbers, test set fixed per design): mean ΔwPearson positive, >=3 of 4 signs positive, none
   CI-clear negative. Raw and drift-absorbed are judged separately; the drift-absorbed arm is the
   one the wide-window measurement predicts can win.
R3 EYE AXIS. The Eye-axis verdict is RE-OPENED only if, at the best (threshold, window): the HR
   and BABIP aux coefficients stop swinging (the published swing is BABIP -0.162/-0.172/-0.673, a
   four-fold move between adjacent windows) AND the six ΔwPearson signs stop sorting perfectly by
   fit window. Both, not either.
CI convention: 95% cluster bootstrap over CARD (cid), 2000 replicates, paired draws across arms.
* marks an interval excluding zero.

§2  WINDOW WIDTH — 1 to 6 seasons, raw and with the season level drift absorbed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW THE TEST SET IS HELD FIXED. A wider window eats the years an out-of-time test would use, so
the test year is PINNED first and windows grow AWAY from it:
  FORWARD  design: test = 2043 (fixed), windows = the last k seasons ending 2042, k = 1..6.
  BACKWARD design: test = 2037 (fixed), windows = the first k seasons starting 2038, k = 1..6.
Within a design every arm is scored on byte-identical test rows at minPA_eval=1000. The 7-season
window (2037-2043) has NO out-of-time block left at all — it is reported at the end on CV and
in-sample only, and cannot be compared to the others on the metric that decides.

DRIFT ABSORPTION, IN TWO VERSIONS. Per season and per channel a per-600 shift is applied to the
training counts BEFORE the seasons are pooled (estimated on a fixed reference cohort, exposure >=
250, so the absorption never moves with the fit threshold). After pooling the season no longer
exists as a covariate, so this is where a season fixed effect has to live.
  ABSORB-resid  centres each season's mean RESIDUAL (observed − predicted under the deployed form).
                THE CORRECT ONE: a fixed effect removes what the model FAILED to explain, and this
                is precisely the quantity the wide-window run measured drifting -2.63…+0.44/600.
  ABSORB-level  centres each season's mean RAW rate. Reported because it is the obvious thing to
                do and it is WRONG: if 2043's cards genuinely hit more homers, that belongs to the
                curve, and centring the raw level deletes real signal along with the drift. The
                gap between the two arms is the size of that mistake.
Either way a card's deviation from its own season's mean — the SHAPE — is untouched.

§2d  WHY THE ABSORBED ARM MOVES SO LITTLE — the mechanism, not a shrug
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The absorbed and raw arms agree to the 4th decimal almost everywhere above. That is a RESULT with
a mechanism, not a failed implementation: a season level shift is a CONSTANT added to every card's
rate in that season, so it reaches a card's POOLED rate only through that card's season MIX. Cards
with the same mix all move by the same amount, and a common shift is absorbed by the fit's
intercept — leaving predictions unchanged up to a constant, which weighted Pearson cannot see.
Only the DIFFERENTIAL part (cards weighted toward different seasons) can move a slope. Measured on
the 6-season window 2038-2043, minPA_eval=1000 rows:

§4  CLUSTER INFLATION FACTOR — measured for THESE statistics, on THESE test sets
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A card contributes two rows (vL and vR) whose errors are correlated, so a row bootstrap understates
the interval. The factor is NOT a constant: it has measured ~3.1x on one statistic and ~1.07x on
another in this project. Reported as SD(cluster bootstrap over cid) / SD(iid row bootstrap).

  panel                              role       rows  cards  rows/card   wPearson    top26

  OOT-BACK-A 2042+2043→2037..        PITCHER     188     78       2.41      1.04x    0.94x
  OOT-FWD 2040+2041→2042..           HITTER      173     74       2.34      1.15x    1.07x
  OOT-FWD 2040+2041→2042..           PITCHER     149     67       2.22      1.08x    1.15x
  OOT-BACK-B 2040+2041→2037..        HITTER      189     84       2.25      1.20x    1.11x
  OOT-BACK-B 2040+2041→2037..        PITCHER     149     68       2.19      1.02x    0.97x

Every interval in §1-§3 uses the CLUSTER version. Nothing here assumes a factor; the numbers above
are what these statistics actually carry on these test sets.

READING: on THESE statistics the factor is small — 1.02-1.22x on wPearson and 0.94-1.15x on top-26,
against 2.2-2.5 rows per card. It is not the ~3.1x seen elsewhere in the project, and on top-26 it
is essentially 1.0 (a discrete 1/26-grained statistic whose bootstrap spread is dominated by which
cards land in the top 26, not by within-card correlation). The reason wPearson clusters more than
top-26: vL and vR of one card share a rating vector, so their prediction ERRORS move together, and
Pearson integrates every row's error while top-26 only counts membership flips. Nothing in the
conclusions turns on the factor — it is reported because assuming one would have been wrong in
both directions depending on which statistic you assumed it for.

§5  DOES THE SWEEP BEAR ON THE 'NO HEADROOM LEFT' READING?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The per-event flex ceiling spends 21 (hitter) / 15 (pitcher) parameters and currently loses to the
deployed curves out of time on 4 of 10 channels — plausibly a parameter-cost artefact that a larger
sample would relieve. That reading and this run share one dependency: whether the fit is
sample-starved. §1's answer to R1 is the direct evidence, and it transfers with a caveat — a
flexible form has MORE to gain from extra rows than a 5-parameter curve does, so a null here bounds
but does not settle the ceiling question. What it does settle: if the deployed curves cannot use the
extra rows at all, 'the ceiling loses on parameter cost' cannot be repaired by lowering the bar
alone. The ceiling's own threshold sweep is a separate measurement and is NOT run here.
