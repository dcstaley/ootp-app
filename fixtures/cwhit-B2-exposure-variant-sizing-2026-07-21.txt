╔═══ 0. WHICH SIDE OF FABLE'S BASELINE-vs-FIELD LINE IS THIS? (stated, not decided) ═══╗
  The doctrine: variants are ALWAYS scored and counted; they are excluded only from
  RATING-SCALING BASELINES. So the classification decides the policy, and the classification is
  precisely the open question. The reading this tool would argue for, with its reasons:

    ⇒ IT IS A FIELD, not a rating-scaling baseline. Three reasons, in decreasing strength:
      1. WHAT IT ESTIMATES. `computeBaseline` answers "what handedness does a card actually MEET?"
         — a usage-weighted composition of the population that takes the mound / stands in the box.
         That is the definition of a FIELD. A rating-scaling baseline answers a different question:
         "in what units was the model fit?" Nothing here re-scales a rating.
      2. WHAT IT IS SUBTRACTED FROM. `deployment = logit(realized) − logit(leagueBaseline)` sets
         the baseline directly against a MEASURED, VARIANT-INCLUSIVE population. A convention leg
         and a measurement leg cannot be differenced; both legs of a difference must estimate the
         same population. Whatever the classification, THIS subtraction is mixed.
      3. ITS OWN DOCUMENTATION. exposure.ts:6-9 calls it "the role-agnostic top-X FIELD" and cites
         the pool-role-agnostic-topx principle — the same principle FIELD_N cohorts are built on.

    THE COUNTER, recorded because it is not empty: the league baseline is CACHED on
    `${activeModelId}|${catalogSource}` with no tournament in the key (server.ts:790) and is
    env-invariant by construction — it behaves like a fixed CONVENTION the deployment shift is
    expressed in, not like a per-pool estimate. §1c tests that env-invariance empirically.
    NOT DECIDED HERE. Sized below either way, because the subtraction is mixed under both readings.
