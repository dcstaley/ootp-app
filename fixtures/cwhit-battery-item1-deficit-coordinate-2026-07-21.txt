THE CONTROL IS WHAT MAKES THIS READABLE. Each per-opponent figure is printed beside the
SCALAR figure (opponents collapsed to their mean, one prediction per card — the machinery
the program already had). The DIFFERENCE between them is the entire curvature term, i.e.
everything the per-opponent integration adds.

  channel     curvature term (per-opponent MINUS scalar), iron -> diamond
  pit K9      +0.004  +0.003  +0.004  +0.004  +0.004
  pit HR9     -0.233  -0.229  -0.237  -0.257  -0.260
  pit BABIP   -0.009  -0.017  -0.017  -0.019  -0.031

1. ON K THE CURVATURE TERM IS ~0.004 AND FLAT. The measured K need varies 1.04 -> 1.82.
   The integration adds roughly one part in 400 of the variation that has to be explained.
   Whatever produces gold's break, it is not opponent-distribution curvature on K.

2. THE CANDIDATE K COORDINATE IS ITSELF FLAT: 1.108 / 1.104 / 1.103 / 1.101 / 1.100.
   It moves by 0.008 across the whole ladder while the need moves by 0.78. It cannot
   produce the tier variation at all, let alone the non-monotone shape.

3. HR IS WRONG IN MAGNITUDE AND IN DIRECTION. Candidate 2.90 -> 2.36 (monotone falling)
   against a measured need of ~1.19 that is flat-to-slightly-rising. Off by roughly a
   factor of two, and sloping the wrong way.

4. BABIP IS WRONG IN SHAPE. Candidate 1.56 -> 1.42, monotone falling. The need rises
   1.26 -> 1.40 through silver then falls to 1.01 / 0.91. No monotone coordinate reproduces
   a rise-then-fall.

WHAT THIS RULES OUT. The premise item 3 made plausible — that gold's need is visible in the
SECOND moment of the opponent field where it is invisible in the first — does not survive
being computed. Gold IS a dispersion outlier (item 3, 6 of 9 channels), but propagating that
dispersion through the model's own curves produces essentially nothing on K, and the wrong
thing on HR and BABIP. Dispersion is real; its modelled consequence is not the need.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE (per the pre-registered reading of a miss). A miss is ambiguous
between WRONG CURVATURE (the curves' shape is off, so the integration propagates dispersion
incorrectly) and MISSING INTERACTION (there is real matchup structure the one-sided core
cannot express at all). Nothing here separates those. The pre-registered discriminator —
cross-pool same-card contrasts from the multi-format corpus, framed as opponent-frame
measurements (do high-K pitchers' K rates move more than low-K pitchers' when the opposition
changes?) — is what separates them, and it is triggered by this result.

NOT DONE / CAVEATS
· Eligible-pool UNIFORM weighting only. The realized-usage weighting (ruling 3) has not been
  run; it feeds item 4 and cohort arbitration. It is unlikely to rescue a 0.004 curvature
  term on K, but that is an expectation, not a measurement.
· Pools are NESTED, so tier rows are not independent samples.
· The degenerate identity (mean-over-j of the shift recovers the scalar shift) and the
  crossing map are pinned in tests/battery-deficit.test.ts, as is the fact that the curvature
  gap is non-zero on the deployed curves — so "the term is small" is a measurement, not a
  bug in the integration.
