################################################################################
### VERDICT ON THE ASYMMETRY:  EXPLAINED
################################################################################

  CAUSE: OPPOSING-BATTER COMPOSITION, resolved by PITCHER hand. The deployed pitching
  prediction contains NO opponent term — predictPitching() sees only the pitcher's own
  ratings — while the opposition a line was thrown to differs enormously by matchup cell.

   1. Opposing avoid-K by cell (PA-weighted, switch-hitters resolved to the hand they bat from):
      same-side matchups face batters -6.37 avoid-K points weaker than opposite-side ones.
   2. The residual tracks it: same−opposite K residual level gap -6.99/600, CI [-9.53, -5.15],
      p=0.000; correlation with the opposition gap +0.80 across seasons, same sign in 33/33 league-seasons.
      The fitted slope is +0.842 K/600 per opposing rating point — 1.14× what the model's OWN
      hitter K curve says a rating point is worth. It is the missing opponent term, at full size.
   3. The two arms carry that effect in DIFFERENT PROPORTIONS: the vR arm is 52% same-side
      matchups, the vL arm 20%. And because STU vR ≠ STU vL (mean gap +3.9 for RHP, -3.9 for LHP),
      the mixture lands at DIFFERENT PLACES on the Stuff axis in each arm — a level effect
      converted into a shape difference.
   4. REMOVE IT AND THE ASYMMETRY IS GONE: with the matchup-cell level removed, vR−vL cubic goes
      -1.0668 → -0.0175 (98% of it), CI [-0.999, +0.404], p=0.770. The arms converge to
      vR -0.5148 vs vL -0.4973. Stratifying instead of correcting gives the same answer:
      the vR−vL cubic computed WITHIN pitcher hand and BF-averaged is -0.1349 (p=0.677).

  There is no hand-specific curve behaviour left to explain. This is consistent with the
  mechanism argument that closed the decision: the platoon difference is already in the
  ratings, and nothing in the engine converts ratings to outcomes differently by hand.

### WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE POOLED CUBIC:  NOT CONTAMINATED — AND ATTENUATED, NOT INFLATED
################################################################################

  The fear was that the pooled -0.4983 is partly the same artifact. It is not. Removing the
  composition makes the pooled cubic STRONGER and CLEANER, not weaker:

     raw (the artifact's number)                    -0.4983  [-1.268, +0.001]           0.050  COVERS 0
     − matchup-cell level                           -0.5999  [-1.193, -0.362]           0.000  CI-CLEAR
     − matchup-cell × season level                  -0.6001  [-1.208, -0.358]           0.000  CI-CLEAR
     − linear opposing-avoid-K term                 -0.6409  [-1.210, -0.354]           0.000  CI-CLEAR

  The composition was ADDING NOISE, not signal: the BF-weighted residual variance falls from
  31.9 to 14.8 (K/600)² once the matchup cells are centred — the composition is 54% of the
  whole K residual, dwarfing anything the curve shape is doing. Its effect on the pooled cubic
  was to widen the CI to the point of covering zero (raw p=0.050), not to create the estimate.

  SO THE POOLED CUBIC IS USABLE — WITH THREE CAVEATS THAT ARE NOT ABOUT THE HAND SPLIT:
   (i)  IT RESTS ON ~10 EFFECTIVE CARDS. The Kish ESS over CARDS of the cubic's own information
        is 9.9 of 70 (the artifact's quoted 94.6 is a ROW-grain ESS, and the bootstrap
        resamples cards, not rows). Craig Kimbrel alone holds 26% of it. A cubic coefficient is
        -16.2 K/600 at z=+3, so its value is set by the two or three cards out there.
   (ii) A CUBIC IS NOT ESTABLISHED AS THE FORM. On the corrected residual the QUARTIC beats the
        cubic on all three cross-validations (LOCO 13.17 vs 13.34), and trimming to |z|≤1.75
        cuts the coefficient to 46% of its value. What is established is only that the leftover
        shape is HIGHER-ORDER THAN QUADRATIC and lives in the Stuff tails.
   (iii) THE FIRST-BEST FIX IS NOT A CURVE TERM AT ALL. The largest measurable defect on this line
        is the missing opponent term (54% of the residual variance, a -6.99/600 matchup gap).
        Fitting a cubic on top of an uncorrected residual spends curve parameters absorbing
        exposure. Note the deployed form is fit on 2042+2043 whose matchup mixture is a property
        of THAT window's rosters and usage — the same drift the pool-turnover argument warns about.

  WHAT WOULD SETTLE THE REMAINING QUESTIONS
   • For the ~10-effective-card problem: cards outside the current Stuff tail, or the
     cwhitstats corpus (100s of instances per format at 1000s of IP) which resolves the elite
     tail this corpus cannot. Nothing inside 'League Files' can fix it — 7 seasons of the same
     70 cards buy ~1.2–1.3× the information of one season (§10).
   • For cubic-vs-quartic-vs-something-else: refit the K channel WITH an opponent term present
     and re-measure the leftover shape. Until the opponent term exists, every shape estimate on
     this line is measured on a residual that is 54% exposure.
   • For the hand question: closed. It is explained, and the explanation is measurable, sized,
     and removable.

## 7e. CANDIDATE 1 — THE OPPOSING-BATTER POPULATION, RESOLVED BY PITCHER HAND
################################################################################

     A switch-hitter bats RIGHT against a LHP and LEFT against a RHP, so 'the RHB population'

## 11. THE "OUT-OF-TIME REPLICATION" IS LARGELY ARITHMETIC
################################################################################

     497 out-of-sample rows; 451 of them (98.4% of OOS BF) belong to a card×arm cell that
     the fit window also contains. Ratings never change, so that cell's residual is close to a
     constant. CARRY-FORWARD TEST — overwrite each OOS row's residual with its own cell's
     IN-SAMPLE mean (zero new information) and recompute:
                                               POOLED         vR         vL
        actual OOS cubic                      -0.4714    -0.8623    +0.2226
        carry-forward (no new info)           -0.5929    -0.8674    -0.0581
        in-sample cubic                       -0.5414    -0.8690    +0.0227

     BF-weighted correlation between a cell's IN-SAMPLE residual and its OUT-OF-SAMPLE residual:
        r = 0.864 over 97 card×arm cells ⇒ 75% of the "new" measurement was
        already determined by the in-sample one.
     The artifact's headline — vR -0.8690 in-sample vs -0.8623 out-of-sample, "a 0.8% difference" —
     is therefore not a strong replication result. Two estimates that share 98% of their cards and
     read the SAME unchanging ratings SHOULD agree closely; agreeing is the null, not the finding.
     The closeness of the agreement (0.8%) is if anything a warning that the two windows are
     nearly the same measurement, not two of them.
