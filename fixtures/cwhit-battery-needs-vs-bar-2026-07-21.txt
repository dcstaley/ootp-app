################################################################################
READING
################################################################################

0. THE BF>=600 COLUMN REPRODUCES THE COMMITTED NEEDS EXACTLY — iron 1.82, bronze
   1.62, silver 1.48, gold 1.78, diamond 1.04, at N = 142/156/121/91/36. That is
   the check that this is the same measurement, not a near-neighbour of it: the
   only thing changed across the three blocks is the builder's `minBf`.

1. THE HEADLINE — THE NEEDS ARE FLOOR-STABLE FOR FOUR TIERS, AND GOLD IS THE ONE
   THAT MOVES.
     iron    1.82 -> 1.83 -> 1.84   (drift +0.02 over a 600->2000 floor)
     bronze  1.62 -> 1.64 -> 1.66   (+0.03)
     silver  1.48 -> 1.47 -> 1.40   (-0.07)
     gold    1.78 -> 1.63 -> 1.63   (-0.15, then flat)
     diamond 1.04 -> 0.85 -> (N=4)  — INSUFFICIENT DATA, see item 3
   Every 600<->2000 pair that can be read has overlapping bootstrap CIs, so no
   single tier's move is CI-clear. Iron and bronze are stable to a degree that
   leaves nothing to argue about: the point estimate moves less than a third of
   its own standard error while N is roughly halved.

2. GOLD'S MOVE IS THE MATERIAL RESULT, AND IT RUNS TOWARD THE OTHER TIERS.
   Gold's need falls 1.78 -> 1.63 between BF>=600 and BF>=1000 and then does not
   move again (1.63 at BF>=2000, N=43, still a usable cell). Two things make this
   worth stating plainly rather than filing under noise:
     (a) THE ORDERING CHANGES. At BF>=600 gold sits ABOVE bronze and second only
         to iron (silver 1.48 < bronze 1.62 < gold 1.78 < iron 1.82) — that
         inversion against the gap ordering IS the gold anomaly. At BF>=1000 and
         BF>=2000 gold falls BELOW bronze and lands on it: 1.63 vs bronze 1.64,
         and 1.63 vs bronze 1.66. The anomaly does not vanish (gold's gap is 15.9
         vs bronze's 22.8, so an equal need is still off-curve) but it shrinks to
         roughly half its BF>=600 size.
     (b) THE MECHANISM IS VISIBLE IN §4. Gold's OBSERVED K9 spread collapses as
         the floor rises — SD 1.61 -> 1.28 -> 1.06 — while iron (1.53 -> 1.51)
         and bronze (1.43 -> 1.61) do not. A slope of obs~pred is a spread ratio;
         gold's lightly-used cards are what supply its extra observed spread, and
         they are exactly the cards a usage floor removes. Gold's retention is
         also the lowest of the four readable tiers (0.47 at BF>=2000).
   VERDICT ON GOLD: this is material evidence that PART of the gold anomaly is
   selection-on-outcome rather than a pool property. It is not proof — the CIs
   overlap (600 [1.63,1.91] vs 2000 [1.41,1.77]) and the cells are nested — but
   the move is one-directional, saturates immediately at the first floor step,
   sits on top of a mechanism that is independently visible in the spread, and
   changes the tier ORDERING that the ramp debate turns on. It should not be
   reported as "gold's need is 1.78" without this caveat attached.

3. DIAMOND CANNOT CARRY A VERDICT AT ANY RAISED FLOOR — INSUFFICIENT DATA.
   N = 36 -> 19 -> 4. At BF>=1000 the cell is THIN (N=19, below the pre-registered
   N>=15 bar only marginally, but its CI is [0.64,1.09] — a full 0.45 wide); at
   BF>=2000 it is DEAD (N=4, no slope computed). The apparent -0.19 move from
   1.04 to 0.85 is reported for completeness and is NOT a drift claim. Diamond
   retains 11% of its cards at BF>=2000, by far the worst of the five, which is
   the same fact as "diamond formats are played less", not a finding about K.
   Nothing in this artifact says anything about whether diamond's 1.04 is real.

4. THE POOLED SLOPE IS FLAT ACROSS FLOORS. With tier fixed effects the within-tier
   spacing deficit reads 1.63 [1.58,1.69] at BF>=600, 1.62 [1.57,1.68] at BF>=1000
   and 1.65 [1.58,1.71] at BF>=2000 (the last over four tiers, diamond having
   dropped out). The aggregate quantity the ramp is fit against does not depend on
   the bar. What the bar moves is the DISTRIBUTION of the need across tiers — i.e.
   the very thing the ramp's shape is estimated from — not its level.

5. THE CONFOUND, STATED. RAISING THE FLOOR CHANGES THE SAMPLE. Each higher-floor
   cell is a nested subset of the 600 cell, so drift confounds selection-on-outcome
   with reduced N, and the two are not separable from a point estimate. The
   bootstrap CIs are how a reader separates them: a move that stays inside both
   intervals while N halves is what "reduced N" looks like, and a move that
   persists at a fixed size across two floor steps while the CI widens (gold) is
   what selection looks like. Neither is proven here. The nesting also means the
   three columns are NOT independent replications.

6. WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH. It does not say whether BF>=600 is the right bar
   (that was settled separately as the noise-equivalent of the old IP>=150 floor),
   it does not re-fit the ramp at any floor, and it does not attribute gold's
   residual off-curve position to any cause. It measures one thing: how much of
   each per-tier need survives raising the usage floor.

(end of artifact — battery NEEDS-vs-DEPTH robustness, per-tier K9 slope at BF>=600/1000/2000)
