  Every model comparison this project has run is scored in wOBA space. Per-EVENT predictive
  accuracy had never been measured and there was no per-event ceiling, so "production is at the
  ceiling" meant only "we predict wOBA about as well as this data allows" — nothing about
  whether we get there for the right reasons. wOBA weights events very unequally (a HR is ~4x a
  single; a K carries no weight of its own and acts solely by removing a ball in play), so a
  LARGE event error can be cancelled by a SMALL one in value terms and the wOBA-level metrics
  are blind to both. This artifact measures the event layer directly.

################################################################################
# PART 2 — THE CANCELLATION MAP: per-rating decomposition of the conditional wOBA bias
################################################################################

  For EVERY rating, the CONDITIONAL (all of the role's ratings held fixed) wOBA bias per SD is
  decomposed EXACTLY into its per-channel contributions. The decomposition is exact row-by-row
  and a conditional slope is a LINEAR operator on the same design, so the channel slopes sum to
  the composite slope — the closure residual is printed and is floating-point only.

  Residual convention: DEPLOYED PREDICTION − OBSERVED. For HITTERS positive = the model says he
  produced MORE than he did = OVER-credit. For PITCHERS the quantity is wOBA ALLOWED, so
  positive = the model says he allowed MORE than he did = UNDER-credit. Same arithmetic,
  mirrored meaning — do not blur the two.

  TRUE frame throughout (the card's REAL hit-by-pitches on the observed side). The harness's own
  target credits a fixed 6 HBP/600 on BOTH sides, so the HBP channel cancels inside it and is
  invisible to every harness metric; that is a property of the metric, not of the model.

  THE RANKING STATISTIC: Σ|channel contributions| ÷ |net composite|. A ratio near 1 means the
  composite IS the channel error. A large ratio means large opposite-signed channel errors that
  nearly cancel — the composite looks clean while specific player types are mis-valued.

  denominator than a typical resample does. Read those cells as "the composite is uninformative
  about the legs here", not as a calibrated multiple.

################################################################################
# PART 2b — THE MEASURED CLUSTER-INFLATION FACTOR (not assumed)
################################################################################

  A card contributes a vL and a vR row driven by the same unchanging ratings and the same fit
  error, so a ROW bootstrap treats non-independent rows as independent and runs too narrow. The
  standing estimates differ by statistic (~3.1x on one, ~1.07x on another), so this artifact
  MEASURES it for its own statistics rather than assuming a factor:

  statistic                                             cluster width    row width  inflation
  hitter composite conditional wOBA slope / SD EYE            1.76e-3      1.77e-3      0.99x
  hitter K-leg conditional contribution / SD EYE              8.76e-4      7.12e-4      1.23x
  pitcher composite conditional wOBAA slope / SD STU          3.04e-3      2.75e-3      1.10x
  pitcher BABIP-leg conditional contribution / SD STU         1.71e-3      1.35e-3      1.26x
  hitter K per-event Δr (ceiling − deployed), BWD-A           1.10e-2      1.01e-2      1.08x

  Every interval in this artifact is the CLUSTER one. The inflation is statistic-specific — which
  is exactly why it is measured per statistic and not applied as a constant.

################################################################################
# PART 3 — WHICH CARDS PAY FOR THE CANCELLATION
################################################################################

  The errors cancel FOR THE AVERAGE PROFILE — that is what a conditional linear slope measures.
  They cannot cancel for every profile: each leg is a different function of the whole rating
  vector, so wherever the legs' curvature or their dependence on a SECOND rating differs, the
  cancellation breaks and the card is mis-valued while the composite still looks clean.

  METHOD, stated before the numbers:
   1. take the worst ratings from Part 2 (by cancellation ratio, restricted to cells whose GROSS
      is materially larger than the resolution of the data — a huge ratio on a tiny gross is a
      near-zero-over-near-zero artifact and is excluded by requiring the two dominant legs to be
      CI-clear);
   2. name the two dominant OPPOSED legs A (positive) and B (negative);
   3. per card, IMBALANCE = (A − Ā) + (B − B̄) — the anchor-free failure of that pair to offset
      (a uniform level shift is absorbed by the baseline/anchor, so only the deviation matters);
   4. regress IMBALANCE on all of the role's ratings to find which axis MODULATES the failure —
      that axis, together with the Part-2 rating, names the affected SHAPE;
   5. report the shape's per-card wOBA error and its movement in a VALUE RANKING (rank by
      predicted, compare to rank by observed) — ranking is what the app delivers.

################################################################################
# PART 4 — WHAT THIS DOES *NOT* SAY
################################################################################

  No correction is proposed or fitted here, and the results should be read against the two
  failures that motivated the artifact:
   · fixing ONE leg of a cancelling set made the composite WORSE — the remaining legs were
     balanced against the one that was removed (fixtures/hitter-eyeaug-2026-07-25.txt);
   · fixing the whole APPARENT set also failed, because the set was six channels not four, and
     because at ~75 distinct cards per window the smaller terms are UNDER-DETERMINED —
     coefficients swung -0.16 / -0.17 / -0.67 across adjacent windows while the well-determined
     ones stayed stable (fixtures/hitter-eye-composite-2026-07-26.txt §2).
  The sample sizes printed beside every row in this artifact are the same ones. A map is what
  this data supports.

  ── THE SAMPLE SIZE IS A CONFIGURATION CHOICE, NOT A PROPERTY OF THE DATA ──
  Reported to this run after it was designed, and it MATTERS for how Part 4 is read: the ~75
  distinct cards per window is a consequence of minPA/minBF = 1000 (the deployed model's own
  training threshold, which this artifact inherits so that "in frame" means the fit set), NOT of
  the corpus. The 2042+2043 window holds 162 distinct hitter cards and 155 pitcher cards BEFORE
  filtering; at PA ≥ 500 it is 94 / 81 and at PA ≥ 250 it is 111 / 90.
  NOTHING here was re-run at another threshold (that is a separate task), so every number above
  stands as measured at 1000. What would change if the threshold were relaxed:
   · the UNDER-DETERMINATION verdict on the smaller cancelling legs would need re-testing — it is
     the one conclusion in this artifact that rests directly on card count, and a ~50% increase
     in distinct cards is exactly the regime where a coefficient that swings four-fold between
     adjacent windows might stabilise. Treat "a fix is under-determined" as PROVISIONAL.
   · the per-event CEILING would move UP, and not uniformly: the flex design spends 21 (hitter) /
     15 (pitcher) parameters, so it is the side of the comparison that gains most from more rows.
     Several negative-Δr channels here (where the flex fit is BEATEN by the deployed curve) are
     plausibly artifacts of that parameter cost rather than genuine absence of headroom.
   · the CANCELLATION MAP itself should be far more robust — it is a decomposition of a fitted
     model's residual, exact row-by-row, not an estimate that needs cards to converge. More cards
     would tighten the CIs but the leg STRUCTURE is arithmetic.
   · the lower-PA cards are by construction less-used cards, so a relaxed threshold changes the
     POPULATION as well as the sample size (more noise per card, and a different card mix). That
     is a real design question, not a free win.

  ── SCOPE, COST, AND SPECIFICATION NOTES (what took the time, what was ambiguous) ──
  COMPLETE: all three requested deliverables — the per-event headroom table (Part 1), the
  cancellation ranking over ALL NINE ratings in both frames (Part 2), and the affected-profile
  analysis (Part 3). Nothing was dropped.
  WHAT COST THE TIME: not the fitting — every fit here is a small weighted least squares and the
  whole run is minutes. The cost was (a) establishing that this artifact's decomposition
  reproduces the published EYE numbers before trusting it anywhere else, and (b) the nested
  cluster bootstraps in Part 2, where the RATIO statistic requires refitting every channel's
  conditional slope inside each of 1200 resamples, per rating, per frame, per role.
  WHAT THE BRIEF UNDER-SPECIFIED, in the order it bit:
   1. "Per-event accuracy" has no single natural metric. Pearson is the project's standing choice
      and is affine-invariant — which is precisely why it CANNOT see the cancelling biases Part 2
      is about. Reporting Pearson alone would have made the two halves of this artifact
      contradict each other. Part 1b exists to reconcile them and was not in the brief.
   2. "Headroom" is direction-ambiguous once the ceiling can LOSE. A 21-term flex fit beaten out
      of time by a 3-parameter curve is not a ceiling at all; several channels are in that state
      here, and the sign convention had to be chosen so that reads as "no headroom", not as
      "negative headroom" (which would invite the reading that the deployed form is ABOVE the
      ceiling — it is not; the ceiling estimate is simply noisy at this N).
   3. XBH is a fitted SHARE, not an event — the deployed form fits share-of-hits on GAP. It is
      reported here as XBH/600 (the actual event) so the ceiling comparison is like-for-like,
      which means the row mixes the share error with the hits-volume error it rides on.
   4. Hitter HBP is not a modelling failure but a structural one, and it needed its own treatment
      rather than a table row: deployed Pearson is 0.000 BY CONSTRUCTION, so the row is
      uninterpretable next to the others without the ceiling reading beside it.
   5. "Which cards pay" required inventing an operationalisation (Part 3's IMBALANCE + modulating
      axis + median-split shape cells). The brief asked for the outcome without naming a
      construction, and the answer is construction-dependent: a different split (terciles, or a
      fitted interaction surface) would name a related but not identical shape. The DIRECTION of
      the finding is stable across the choices tried; the exact cell boundaries are not, and at
      20-40 cards per cell they cannot be. Read Part 3 as "this kind of card", not as a roster.
