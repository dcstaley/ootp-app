# READING
################################################################################

1. THE AMENDMENT WAS RIGHT, AND IT MATTERS. Conditioning moves the prior a long way:
   pitchers 17.7-28.6% raw -> 25.7-42.6% conditional; hitters 14.8-26.9% -> 21.8-42.3%.
   Roughly a 1.5-1.8x lift, because 19-46% of tournament usage is played by cards that
   CANNOT have a variant and was sitting in the raw denominator. The decomposition
   (raw = eligible-usage-share x conditional propensity) reproduces every raw share EXACTLY,
   so the identity is not approximate — it is the definition, and the two factors are
   separately measurable.

2. THE HOPED-FOR SIMPLIFICATION IS REFUTED. The pre-registered question was whether the
   15-29% raw spread is mostly ELIGIBILITY-MIX variation, leaving a stable conditional p —
   which would have licensed one global number. It does not. Conditioning WIDENS the spread:

       pitchers   raw range 10.9 points  ->  conditional range 16.9 points
       hitters    raw range 12.1 points  ->  conditional range 20.5 points

   Conditional p runs 21.8% to 42.6% across the ten format x role cells — nearly a factor of
   two. A single global p is not supported by this evidence. The eligibility mix was not the
   source of the variation; it was partially MASKING variation that is larger underneath.

3. THE ASYMMETRY SURVIVES CONDITIONING, AND IS STARK ON THE PITCHER SIDE. Like for like:

       pitchers   league conditional p  5.2%   vs tournament 25.7-42.6%   -> 5x to 8x
       hitters    league conditional p 17.4%   vs tournament 21.8-42.3%   -> 1.3x to 2.4x

   So even among cards that CAN have a variant, league play uses them far less than
   tournament play, and much more so for pitchers. This is the like-for-like version of
   Derek's original claim, and it holds. The two legs differ for a REAL reason — league play
   runs on new 100+ cards whose variants are rare — not because of a policy error, which is
   why equalising the policy was the wrong repair.

4. WHY THE CONDITIONING MATTERS, CONCRETELY: the ineligible-class mix is not stable across
   formats. Iron's ineligible play is Live-heavy (14.0%); gold and diamond are Mission-Edition-
   heavy (11.7%, 14.0%); bronze and silver are LE/Clubhouse-heavy. So the raw share's
   denominator is contaminated by a DIFFERENT population in every window, which is exactly
   the failure mode the amendment identified.

5. WHAT THIS SETS UP. The pool-leg mixture weights eligible cards only, so p on the
   conditional scale is the consistent input: ~26-43% (pitchers) / ~22-42% (hitters), not the
   raw 15-29%. Since p is NOT stable, the sizing sweep matters more than expected — if gaps
   and s_K are sensitive across that range, a property-conditioned p is required rather than
   a global constant, per the pre-registered branch.

(end of artifact — conditional presence: no global p is supported)
