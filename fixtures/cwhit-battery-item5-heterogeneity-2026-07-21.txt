# READING — descriptive. Nothing here establishes cause for gold's 1.78 need.

A CAVEAT I HAVE BEEN REPEATING IS WRONG, AND THIS CORRECTS IT. The eligible POOLS are
nested (iron subset of bronze subset of ...), and I have flagged tier rows as
non-independent on that basis several times. At the WELL-SAMPLED OBSERVED level they are
very nearly DISJOINT: only 4 / 4 / 1 / 1 cards are shared with the tier below, i.e. 1-3%.
Different cards actually get played in each format. So per-tier observed statistics are
much closer to independent samples than the nesting suggested. The nesting caveat applies
to CATALOG-derived pool statistics (items 2 and 3), not to these.

GOLD IS THE MOST HETEROGENEOUS TIER ON EVERY MEASURE HERE.

 1. WIDEST K9 SPREAD. SD of observed K9: iron 1.52, bronze 1.40, silver 1.37, GOLD 1.60,
    diamond 0.92. Gold is the widest despite having only 94 cards against iron's 147.

 2. LARGEST INTERNAL GAP. Largest gap between consecutive K9 values in the middle 80%:
    iron 0.310, bronze 0.190, silver 0.250, GOLD 0.550, diamond 0.310 — roughly double any
    other tier, sitting at [5.83, 6.38]. Suggestive of two groups rather than one
    continuum, though a single gap in N=94 is not evidence of bimodality on its own.

 3. ITS LOW-K SUBGROUP IS UNUSUALLY DISPERSED. SD within the low tercile: iron 0.90,
    bronze 0.92, silver 0.89, GOLD 1.48, diamond 0.67. Gold's low end is ~60% wider than
    any other tier's.

 4. AND ITS USAGE PROFILE IS INVERTED. Mean IP by K9 tercile:
        iron     low  623 · mid 1055 · high 1167
        bronze   low 1030 · mid  977 · high 1059
        silver   low  669 · mid  770 · high 1569
        GOLD     low  918 · mid 1129 · high  549      <- high-K cards used LEAST
        diamond  low  279 · mid  271 · high  337
    In every other tier the high-K group is used as much or more. In gold it is used
    roughly half as much as its own mid group. Gold's high-K end is thin in innings.

 5. THE ROLE SPLIT DOES NOT EXPLAIN IT. Starter-minus-reliever K9 gap: iron -0.72,
    bronze -0.63, silver -0.65, GOLD -0.39, diamond +0.04. Gold's role gap is the SMALLEST
    of the first four, so the starter/reliever mix is not what makes gold spread out. Gold
    also has the lowest starter share by count (48.9%) but the highest starter IP share
    (71.6%) of those four — its starters carry more of the innings while being fewer.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SUPPORT. It establishes that gold is internally different in
kind, not just in level: widest spread, largest internal gap, most dispersed low end, and an
inverted usage profile — and that none of this is the starter/reliever split. It does NOT
establish that any of these produce the 1.78 need. In particular, item 4 (realized vs
eligible opposition) is the natural place to test whether the inverted usage profile matters,
since a calibration slope is fit over exactly these cards with exactly these innings.

CAVEATS. N=94 for gold and N=36 for diamond; the tercile splits are 31 and 12 respectively.
Colliding (Name,VAL,VLvl,Hand) keys are excluded per tier (counts in the table). The bar is
BF>=600. Diamond's whole distribution is narrower (range 4.78-8.58 against gold's 2.30-10.00),
so its low SD is partly a range effect.
